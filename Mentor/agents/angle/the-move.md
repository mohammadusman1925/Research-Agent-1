# The move: Angle Swap

**Keep the moment. Change the reason.**

The scene stays exactly where it was. What moves is why they were never fixed, and everything downstream shifts to stay consistent with it.

---

## What an Angle is

The emotional reason the situation makes someone buy.

It has two halves and both have to be written down.

| | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| The angle | The territory. A few words. "Surgery wait reversal", "gender gap in diagnosis" |
| The angle point | The actual sentence that carries it, verbatim |

**An angle with no angle point is a topic, not an angle.** The angle point is the sentence that makes a reader feel the ad was written about them, and it is usually the thing that is missing when a concept feels vague.

If the client card has an angle with no angle point, ask for it before doing anything else.

---

## The layer table

| Layer | Moves? |
| --- | --- |
| ICP | Sometimes, as a consequence. Note it, do not treat it as a second variable |
| Use Case | **No. The scene does not move. That is what makes this an angle swap** |
| **Angle, belief shift** | **Yes. This is the test** |
| **Angle, emotion** | **Yes. The emotion is the angle. It has to move with it** |
| Awareness | No, but hold it deliberately. See the warning below |
| Bucket | No |
| Format | No |
| Length | No. Within 10 percent |
| Audience, budget, page, landing page | No. Identical |

**Two rows move, and they are really one row.** The angle is the belief shift and the emotion together.

### The awareness warning

Reframe angles drift solution aware without anyone deciding to. If awareness drifts, the landing page stops matching the ad and the test is measuring the mismatch instead of the angle.

Hold awareness where the control had it, on purpose, and say so in the brief.

---

## The beat map

By number, for a typical long-form script:

**Changes: 1, 6, 8, 10, 12, 17, 19**

| Beat | Why it moves |
| --- | --- |
| 1 Hook | The hook carries the angle's promise, so a new belief shift needs a new hook |
| 6 Failed Attempts | The old path has to now show whatever the new reframe says was missed |
| 8 Bridge | The same person can arrive. What they say or ask is the whole angle |
| **10 Mechanism** | **The heart of it. Same problem, new cause** |
| 12 Differentiation | Why the alternatives failed has to match the new cause |
| 17 CTA | Names the reader the new angle is for |
| 19 Echo Close | Echoes the hook, so it follows |

**Never moves: 2, 3, 4, 7, 13, 14, 18.** The narrator, how it started, the losses, the low point, the product, the results and the guarantee.

**Expect six to eight beats.** This is the biggest of the three moves.

---

## Beat 10 is the only honest test

The iteration and the use case swap both leave the mechanism completely alone. The angle swap rewrites it.

**If you set out to change the angle and beat 10 still reads the same, you did not change the angle.** You changed the messenger, or the proof, or the wording. Those are worth testing, but they are not this.

Swapping who delivers the explanation is a proof swap. Swapping what the explanation says is an angle swap.

---

## Where to get the new angle

In order of confidence:

1. **A reframe the account has already won with elsewhere.** Another product, another campaign. Proven argument, new place.
2. **An angle card with no winner behind it.** Written but never run.
3. **The objection bank.** The thing people argue with in the comments is often the belief the next angle has to shift.
4. **The research whitespace.** A desire that ranks high and nobody in the category is naming.

Anything from 3 or 4 is a genuine unknown. Say so rather than presenting it as a safe bet.

---

## Naming

Use Case times Angle is the concept. A new belief shift makes a **new concept, NN.**

---

## Failure modes to watch for

1. **Beat 10 did not move.** The most common one. Covered above.
2. **The scene moved too.** Then it is a new concept from scratch and the comparison against the control is worthless.
3. **Awareness drifted.** Covered above.
4. **The new angle contradicts the brand's mechanism.** If the product physically cannot deliver on the new reframe, kill it before writing. Check the product spec in the client card.
5. **Antagonism.** Reframe angles that blame a group land hard and age badly. Aim the argument at an incomplete explanation, not at people. Check the client card for guardrails on this.

---

## How to read the result

One question: **will they believe a different reason?**

| Outcome | What it means | Next |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Test wins | The old argument was the ceiling. Scale the new one with use case swaps |
| Roughly level | Two arguments both work. That is a real asset. Run both and widen each with scenes |
| Test loses | The original angle is doing the work. Stop hunting for new arguments and go widen the one you have |

Watch the comments as well as the numbers. A reframe either lands hard or gets argued with, and both are information.

Judge on the client's own primary metric, named in the client card.
