# The move: Use Case Swap

**Keep the reason it works. Change the moment it happens in.**

Everything that is their situation moves. Everything that is why the product works stays.

---

## What a Use Case is

The situation the buyer is in at the moment the ad finds them.

If you cannot film it, it is not a use case. It is a demographic.

- "Women 55 to 64 with hip pain" is not a use case. You cannot film that.
- "7am, sitting on the edge of the bed, holding a sock, and she cannot reach her foot" is a use case.

### The four ingredients

All four, or it is not finished.

| Ingredient | The question it answers |
| --- | --- |
| The person | Who is in the frame |
| The place | Where they are |
| The time or trigger | What just happened |
| What is going wrong | Why this moment hurts |

### Write it as a name plus a scene, in one line

`the unsigned paperwork, pre-admission forms on the kitchen bench for three weeks, surgery recommended but gut says no`

### What it is not

| Not the use case | What it actually is |
| --- | --- |
| An age bracket | The ICP. A group of people |
| A diagnosis | The condition |
| A pain cluster | A folder of situations, not a situation |
| The emotion inside the moment | The angle |
| One line pulled out of the scene | The hook |
| Native article, listicle, UGC | The format |

Cluster is the folder. Use case is the scene inside it. Angle is why the scene hurts.

## The layer table

| Layer | Moves? |
| --- | --- |
| ICP | Usually. It follows the scene. A consequence, not a second variable |
| **Use Case** | **Yes. This is the test** |
| Angle, belief shift | No. This is the control |
| Angle, emotion | No, if the new scene is chosen properly |
| Awareness | No |
| Bucket | No |
| Format | No |
| Length | No. Within 10 percent |
| Audience, budget, page, landing page | No. Identical |

**Two rows move.** If more than two move, this is not a use case swap.

---

## The beat map

By number, for a typical long-form script:

**Changes: 1, 4, 6, 7, 14, 17, 19**

| Beat | Why it moves |
| --- | --- |
| 1 Hook | Built out of the scene |
| 4 Pattern | Trim the line the new hook just took, or add the new losses |
| 6 Failed Attempts | The path they were put on, and what they type at 2am |
| 7 Low Point | Only the one tied to the situation. The one at home stays |
| 14 Results | Only the line that resolves the situation |
| 17 CTA | Names the new reader |
| 19 Echo Close | Echoes the hook, so it follows |

**Never moves: 2, 3, 8, 10, 12, 13, 18.** The narrator, how it started, the discovery, the mechanism, the differentiation, the product and the guarantee.

**Expect five to seven beats.** If only two moved, the scene did not really change. If ten moved, the angle moved too and this is no longer a clean test.

A beat that is absent in the control stays absent in the test. Do not fix structural gaps during a swap.

---

## The rule that keeps it clean

**Choose the new scene by the feeling, not the topic.**

Two different things live inside what people loosely call the angle:

| | Moves in a use case swap? |
| --- | --- |
| The belief shift, the reframe | No. Never. It is the control |
| The emotion inside the scene | No, if you pick the new scene carefully |

The emotion lives inside the scene. Move the scene carelessly and the emotion moves with it, and then two things are being tested.

**Bad swap.** Control emotion is dread of something irreversible. New scene is "told she is not sick enough yet". The emotion flips to abandonment. Two variables.

**Good swap.** Same dread, different situation. One variable.

---

## Naming

Use Case times Angle is the concept. So a new scene makes a **new concept, NN, not an iteration.** Keep ITE for changes inside the execution.

---

## Scaling, which is this move repeated

This agent also owns scaling, because scaling is nothing more than this move run three to five times with the angle locked.

| Step | What you do |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | Write the angle point down word for word. It never changes again |
| 2 | Take the scenes out of the winning script itself. They are usually buried at beats 3, 4, 6, 7 and 14 |
| 3 | Rank them against the research by how many people live in each one |
| 4 | Build each one. Only the beats above move |
| 5 | Run three to five, one at a time, same audience and budget and landing page |
| 6 | Two beating baseline means the angle travels. One is luck |
| 7 | Then build a dedicated landing page for that angle |
| 8 | Then raise budget |
| 9 | Then run the winners in other buckets. Exhaustion is per bucket, not per angle |
| 10 | Only look for a new angle when four or five scenes have failed under this one |

**The finding that keeps repeating.** The scenes are already inside the winning script. Research does not invent them. It ranks them and tells you which one has earned the front page.

---

## Failure modes to watch for

1. **The length drifts.** The new version comes back at half the word count. You tested length, not the scene.
2. **The emotion drifts.** Covered above. The most common one and the hardest to see.
3. **Someone fixes a gap at the same time.** Adding proof or an expert while swapping the scene means two changes and no readable result.
4. **Different landing page.** Then the result belongs to the page.
5. **Calling it ITE.** In three months the reporting will not be able to tell a new concept from a tweak.

---

## How to read the result

One question: **which situation do they recognise faster?**

| Outcome | What it means | Next |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Test wins | The new scene is the sharper doorway | It becomes the control. Swap again against it |
| Roughly level | The scene is not the lever. The belief shift is doing the work | Go test the angle |
| Test loses | The original scene is doing real work | Keep it, test a different layer |

Judge on the client's own primary metric, named in the client card. Not on reactions or comments unless the client card says engagement is the goal.
