# The brief: static image

Two things, and nothing else. A headline that stops the right person, and an image description precise enough that the editor invents nothing.

---

## The two steps

### Step 1, nine headlines

**6 to 8 words maximum.** One job: stop the scroll of this specific person and make them want to know more.

For each of the nine, give exactly this:

1. **The headline.**
2. **The pattern it uses.** Named from `Mentor/brain/hook-styles.md`, and from `Mentor/brain/hook-guide.md` if a video framework applies. Name the locked hook style too.
3. **Why it stops this avatar.** One sentence, specific to this person and this moment. Not "it creates curiosity."
4. **Two more versions** of the same idea to choose from.

Then **stop and ask which ones to build.** Do not move to step 2 on your own.

### Step 2, the image descriptions

Only once they have picked. Condensed, not an essay. Use this shape:

```text
HEADLINE
  <exact words, and where they sit in the frame>

VISUAL ROLE
  <level number and name from the hierarchy, and why that level won>

BACKGROUND AND LAYOUT
  <colour, composition, where the eye lands first>

FOCAL SUBJECT
  <the one concrete thing, described so it can be shot or sourced>

SUPPORTING ELEMENTS
  <at most three>

TEXT ON IMAGE
  <headline, plus any callout text, exact wording>

TONE
  <what the frame should feel like>

WHAT NOT TO DO
  <the specific trap for this concept>
```

## The 6 to 8 word rule

Check it against the client's own winning image headlines, listed in the client card. If they are longer or shorter than 6 to 8 words, **the client's evidence wins over this rule.** Write down what their range actually is and use that.

If the client has no winning headlines yet, 6 to 8 is the default.

---

## The hierarchy decides the visual, not taste

Full rules in `Mentor/brain/image-rules.md`. When two ideas compete, the higher number wins.

| Level | Rule | Use when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Exact referent | The headline says this, these, look, or what happened |
| 2 | Specific visible symptom or event | A concrete unusual clue exists. Beats a generic authority portrait |
| 3 | Proof of the surprising result | A chart, scan, record or log creates the belief shift |
| 4 | Authority identity | The credential is the hook and nothing stronger exists |
| 5 | Product or use | Correct use, product difference, or a warm high intent decision |
| 6 | Lifestyle outcome | Only when nothing more concrete exists |

The governing rule: **show the most interesting concrete evidence in the script. The image is a thing, never an idea.**

**Default to level 2 or 3.** If you choose level 5, say why the stronger levels did not apply. Nearly a third of audited winners showed a symptom or consequence. Only a fifth led with an authority portrait.

### Harvest the level 2 candidates from the script

Before you choose, read the client's control script and list every concrete object in it. They are usually buried in the crisis beat, the pattern beat, the low point and the results.

That list is your level 2 shortlist. **Never invent an object that is not in the copy.**

---

## Layout, five rules

1. One large readable outcome or decision headline.
2. The product shown clearly, if it is shown at all.
3. Three mechanisms at most on the main image.
4. A single offer cue.
5. Diagrams for mechanism and education ads. Product renders for conversion ads.

**The rule that matters most:** never combine a dense diagram, a long headline, every feature, a badge, a discount, ratings and multiple claims in one frame. Clean hierarchy is what makes an image work.

---

## Match the awareness stage

Say which stage you are writing for at the top of the brief.

| Stage | The image |
| --- | --- |
| TOF, problem aware or unaware | Lead with the problem or the moment. Product small or absent. No offer cue, or one at most |
| MOF, solution or product aware | The mechanism or the proof. Product natural, in use |
| BOF, most aware | Product forward, proof stacked, offer visible |

**Never show the product before the mechanism has been earned.** On cold traffic it has not been.

---

## Four rules to check everything against

1. Assume the reader does not know the brand and does not care about it.
2. The image must match and reinforce the headline. No decorative pictures.
3. Make them feel understood. That is the whole job.
4. Clear, concise, simple language.

---

## On cleverness

Wordplay and puns are for brand awareness. This is direct response to people with a real problem.

Write to them as an adult and do not be clever at the expense of being clear. A pun aimed at someone in pain reads as a brand that has never felt it.

Check the client card for the audience's age and sophistication before deciding tone. A sceptical, remedy literate audience punishes cleverness harder than a cold one does.

---

## Compliance

Read the guardrails in the client card before writing a word. Every brand has different ones. These are the defaults:

- Never present the product as a substitute for diagnosis or necessary medical care, if it is health adjacent.
- No invented statistics. If a number is not in the research, it does not go on the image.
- Any testimonial claim carries a results may vary line.
- Do not imply credibility that does not exist. If a named expert is an advertorial persona, they stay disclosed as one.
- Carry any compliance flag from the angle card into the brief so the editor and the reviewer both see it.

## Finish by saving it

Write the brief to `Mentor/topics/<brand>/YYYY-MM-DD-static-<concept-slug>.md` and add a line to `Mentor/topics/_index.md`.
