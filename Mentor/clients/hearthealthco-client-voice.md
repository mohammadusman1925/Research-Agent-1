# The client layer

Dell does not just build creative. He reports to a client. So every concept review ends with the client version, and it is not an afterthought.

Two parts, always in this order.

---

## Part 1. Keep in mind

What the client cares about that Dell should be holding in his head while he builds this.

This is where you catch expensive mistakes early. If a concept walks into ground the client has already tested, or into a format he has already asked for, say so before Dell spends a week on it.

Check every concept against these four questions:

1. Has the client already tested this angle?
2. Has the client specifically asked for something in this territory?
3. Does this fit the deadline he is waiting on?
4. Does this sit inside what he is comfortable claiming?

---

## Part 2. Ready to send

Two or three sentences Dell can lift straight into Discord or an email.

**Rules for this block:**

- No jargon. No framework words. No cluster codes, no ICP numbers, no stage numbers, no beat numbers.
- No em dashes. See `writing-rules.md`.
- Lead with the money question: why would this make more sales.
- Say what is new about it, and say honestly whether it is proven or a genuine test.
- A person who has never read a marketing book should understand every word.

**Wrong:**

> This is a Stage 4 mechanism play into an underserved ICP with an open coverage gap on PC04 and a proven MOF shape borrowed from AD 7.

**Right:**

> This one goes after the people who cannot sleep on that side. It is the single biggest thing customers complain about in the reviews, and we have never run an ad about it. It is a new angle so it is a real test, but the format is one that has already worked for us.

## Client card: Pete, HeartHealthCo

Last updated 16 Aug 2026. Update this as Dell learns more.

### Already tested, hip belt

Do not present these as new ideas. He has run them.

- Bone on bone
- Cure before arthritis
- Hip replacement, the before angle

### Asked for, not yet built

He has directly requested these. A concept that lands here gets a warmer reception.

- **Piriformis**
- **Sciatica**

Both are open ground in our own research too. PC18 Sciatica and Nerve Pain holds zero angle cards, so there is nothing written for it yet. Pete also notes there is not much public forum research on piriformis, so the usual voice of customer mining will be thinner than normal. Worth flagging that honestly rather than promising the same depth of research we had for sleep.

### Claim tolerance

Pete is comfortable pushing further than we have been. He is open to NASA research references and surgeon recommendations, and he has said he will push claims harder if needed. He likes the VSL approach.

This means the constraint on bold copy is Meta policy and the compliance flags on the angle cards, not the client. Do not soften a concept on the assumption he will not like it. Do flag genuine compliance risk, especially anything touching the TGA claim or penetration depth.

### Competitor he watches

Daywick. He has followed them from day one and knows their piriformis angle. Assume he will recognise anything that looks like a Daywick copy, and assume he is fine with going into their territory deliberately.

### Deadlines he is waiting on

- Hip belt UGC briefs, due around 23 Aug 2026
- Shoulder belt UGC briefs, due around 30 Aug 2026

He has started receiving hip belt UGC raw footage and is uploading it to Google Drive. He wants to start making short form content from it.

**What this means for concept advice.** UGC is a live production channel now, not a hypothetical. When a concept could run as UGC, say so, because there is footage arriving and briefs are due. This is also the account's first real chance to run the diversification rule from Module 1.1, since everything proven so far is static.

### Two open questions to raise with him when the moment fits

- **The TGA claim.** The shoulder landing page describes the device as TGA registered. Our own research flagged this as unverified. It is live on the page right now, so the exposure exists either way. Worth settling.
- **The ad naming mismatch.** The Meta age and gender export labels ads AD1 to AD7. The creative audit labels them AD 1 to AD 8. They are different schemes over different windows, so the audience data cannot currently be joined to the decoded winners. One export keyed to the creative IDs closes it.
