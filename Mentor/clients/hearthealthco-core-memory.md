# Core Memory: what is working in HeartHealthCo's Facebook ads

Source: `Core_Memory_New_Brand_Facebook_Ads.pdf`, 4 August 2026. 8 unique Facebook posts, 5 with purchase data.

This is the account's own creative core memory. It came before the 16 field DNA reads and it holds things nothing else holds: the named commenters and what they actually objected to, the visual hierarchy rules, and the guardrails.

---

## The whole thing in one paragraph

> We win with treatment experienced midlife women who feel dismissed, exhausted, and close to escalation. They do not want another generic pain product. They want a credible reason everything else failed and one last non invasive step before surgery or more injections. The winning creative mirrors a private daily loss, explains a missed tissue or depth mechanism, shows the glowing wearable clearly, and promises the return of ordinary independence: sleeping on one side, fastening a bra, putting on socks, working, and reaching the shelf, supported by simple at home use and risk reversal.

---

## The five step formula that is working

1. **Mirror a painfully specific daily life loss.** Sleeping on one side, fastening a bra, putting on socks, reaching a shelf, working, getting out of a chair.
2. **Validate the frustration** with doctors, physio, injections, pills, and generic "give it time" advice.
3. **Reveal a missed mechanism.** Treatment has targeted the wrong tissue or the wrong depth.
4. **Present the wearable as a multi modal at home bridge.** Red and near infrared light, heat, vibration, compression.
5. **Offer a real alternative to escalation.** Delay or avoid surgery, regain function, stop organising life around pain.

The market is highly sophisticated. These people already know the diagnosis, have tried several remedies, compare modalities in the comments, and challenge medical claims. Generic pain relief is not enough. The winning message needs a new explanation, a vivid personal outcome, and a reason this device is different from heat packs, TENS, physio, cortisone, PRP, supplements, or surgery.

## The five hooks that work

1. **A high stakes decision.** "Before you book surgery..."
2. **A pattern or social discovery.** "The belt people use instead..."
3. **Authority reversal.** "Three appointments missed it. One specialist connected the dots."
4. **A vivid symptom.** "Three hours of sleep." "Beaten by a sock." "Cannot fasten my bra."
5. **A new mechanism.** "The pain may not be coming only from the bone."

## Visual hierarchy that works

This is the most actionable section in the document and it is not written down anywhere else.

1. One large, readable outcome or decision headline.
2. The product shown clearly with the LEDs active.
3. Three mechanisms at most on the main image.
4. A single offer cue.
5. Medical diagrams for mechanism and education ads. Product renders for conversion ads.

**The rule that matters most:** do not combine a dense diagram, a long headline, every feature, a badge, a discount, ratings, and multiple claims in one frame. The best performing image in the account works because its hierarchy is clean.

Note that the winning creatives here are described as anatomical diagrams with the painful area circled in red, functioning as a "diagnosis reveal" rather than a traditional product ad. That is a real visual concept and it is currently underused.

## Proof language that feels believable

- Small functional wins beat broad promises.
- Time based routines ("20 minutes on the couch") reduce perceived effort.
- "I did not expect it to work" lowers testimonial polish, which raises belief.
- Peer discovery ("hip pain groups", "a forum", "another woman") matches how this audience actually researches.
- Guarantees reduce the fear of trying another failed remedy.

## Awareness by creative type

| Creative type | Awareness level | The job of the ad |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Long personal advertorial | Problem aware to solution aware | Build identification, overturn the old explanation, introduce a new mechanism |
| Medical diagram | Solution aware | Make the hidden tissue and mechanism visible and credible |
| "Before surgery" product ad | Problem and solution aware | Turn urgency into consideration of the device |
| Product feature graphic | Product aware | Show exactly what the buyer gets and how it works |
| Sale or discount graphic | Most aware | Close people who already accept the category and need a reason to act now |

## The objection bank, all ten

1. "I tried red light and felt no difference."
2. "Cortisone, PRP, physio, exercise or TENS already worked for me."
3. "This is just an ad."
4. "Bone on bone is visible on an X ray. Surgery is the real answer."
5. "Is it safe or useful with a tear, nerve damage, rheumatoid arthritis, or another diagnosis?"
6. "Do I wear it directly on skin or over clothing?"
7. "Can I use the hip product on my shoulder?"
8. "Can I wear it while working?"
9. "How quickly should I expect a noticeable change?"
10. "Why is this different from a heat pack or TENS?"

Numbers 6 to 10 are pre purchase uncertainty, not scepticism. They are the cheapest objections to answer and mostly go unanswered.

## Six creative briefs the core memory recommends

Check any new concept against this list first. Some of these are still unbuilt.

1. **One Last Step Before Surgery.** The proven visual hierarchy with a calmer claim: "Before you book the date, understand where the daily pain may be coming from."
2. **The 2AM Test.** Lead with sleep. "If rolling onto your shoulder wakes you at 2AM, this is the part most treatment plans miss." Night time lifestyle visual with a simple joint depth inset.
3. **Back to the Small Things.** Carousel or UGC sequence: bra strap, top shelf, socks, seatbelt, getting out of a chair. The promise is effortless normality, not athletic performance.
4. **Why Heat Fades.** Comparison creative. Surface warmth versus combined modalities, in plain language.
5. **Wear It While You Work.** Straight from Karen's comment. The belt over thin clothing during desk work, with clear usage guidance.
6. **Three Practitioners, One Missing Question.** Keep the identity validation of the perimenopause ad but reduce the gender antagonism. Focus on the completeness of the assessment rather than implying male clinicians cannot understand women.

## Guardrails

- Claims such as "no longer needed surgery", "reverses inflammation", "repairs tissue", and exact penetration depth numbers are strategically powerful but must be substantiated before scaling.
- Never present the device as a substitute for diagnosis or necessary medical care.
- Use comments about tears, nerve damage, rheumatoid arthritis and failed red light as prompts for careful FAQs, not as openings for condition specific promises.
- Preserve scepticism in the comment section. Honest specific answers create more trust than deleting or arguing with dissent.
