# Client card: HeartHealthCo

Australian direct to consumer seller of red light therapy wearables. Two products carry the account: a hip belt and a shoulder belt. Both $149.99 AUD, positioned as 50 percent off $300.

Client contact: Pete. See `../references/client-voice.md` for what he has tested, asked for, and is waiting on.

**The real category.** The brand is not primarily selling red light therapy. It is selling one last credible non invasive step before something irreversible: surgery, repeated injections, or being told to wait indefinitely. Every winning ad is a different route to that one idea.

### The one idea underneath all of them

**Wrong depth, wrong tissue.** The pills, heat packs, stretches and physio all work on the surface. The real problem is deeper: soft tissue starved of blood flow, stuck in a loop of swelling.

Every winner says a version of this. If a new concept does not have a version of it, that is worth talking about.

### What every winner also carries

- Fear as the driving desire, meaning freedom from pain and from danger
- One person's own result as the proof, not statistics
- Static native text, never video
- The product introduced only after the mechanism, never before
- The same four things at once: red light, near infrared, heat, vibration and compression
- The guarantee delivered inside the story as something a person says, never as a footer

| | Hip | Shoulder |

### The finding that surprises people

Engagement and conversion are not the same thing here. The highest volume ad, AD 7, has 8 reactions and zero comments. The most talked about ad, AD 1, has no labelled purchases. The highest ROAS ad, AD 6, has no story at all. Long form storytelling builds belief at the top. Short form and offer ads convert lower down. The funnel works as a system.

## The five structural gaps

These are true across every winner. They come up constantly, so know them cold.

1. **No proof of others.** Not one ad carries a volume number, despite 289 hip reviews, 74 shoulder reviews and a claimed 12,000 customers. This is the cheapest fix in the account.
2. **No expert reacting to the result.** The doctor and nurse characters explain the mechanism but never react to the outcome. We pay for the credential and use half of it.
3. **Every image is text on a plain background.** Zero symptom images, zero proof of result images, despite the copy handing over the sock, the paperwork, the top shelf, the bra strap. Biggest untested opportunity.
4. **No guarantee in any short form ad.** The two ads that carry it are the two most complete.
5. **Every ad is written for women.** Males 65 plus convert at $34.99 CPA, the cheapest segment in the account, with zero creative speaking to them.

A sixth, in Module 1.1's terms: **only one bucket has ever been tested.** Everything proven is static. Video hook rate is 46.1 percent and hold rate is 32.9 percent, both below benchmark. There are four proven Use Case times Angle pairs that have never been run as UGC, video or VSL.

## Two things that are not settled

Say so plainly if either comes up. Do not paper over them.

**The TGA claim.** The shoulder landing page describes the device as TGA registered. The brand intel flagged this as unverified. It is live on the page right now.

**The ad naming mismatch.** The Meta age and gender export labels ads AD1 to AD7 with 300 plus purchases each. The creative audit labels them AD 1 to AD 8 with 15 to 39 purchases each. Different schemes, different windows. The audit's "AD 1 hip" is the DNA read's "AD 7". Audience data therefore cannot be joined to the decoded winners, and the workbook keeps them in separate namespaces on purpose. One export keyed to the creative IDs closes it.

## A caution about the workbook's angle fields

On the 116 angle rows, six fields were assigned by rule rather than read off the source cards, because the source cards do not carry them: `hook_type`, `concept_creative`, `awareness`, `belief_shift_type`, `socproof_primary` and `mechanism_tag`. Every one of those rows says so in its notes.

They feed the Priority ranking. So treat that ranking as a starting point for conversation, not as an answer. If a ranking decides something important, check the underlying angle card first.
