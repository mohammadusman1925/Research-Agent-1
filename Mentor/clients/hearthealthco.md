## Structural gaps

True of every ad in the account. Test each on its own, never inside another test.

1. **No proof of others.** Beat 16 absent everywhere, despite 289 hip reviews, 74 shoulder reviews and a claimed 12,000 customers. Cheapest fix in the account.
2. **No expert reacting to the result.** Beat 15 absent. The doctor and nurse characters explain the mechanism but never react to the outcome.
3. **Every image is text on a plain background.** Zero symptom images, zero proof of result images. Biggest untested opportunity.
4. **No guarantee in any short form ad.**
5. **Every ad is written for women**, while males 65 plus are the cheapest segment.
6. **Only one bucket has ever been tested.** Everything proven is static. Video hook rate 46.1 percent, hold rate 32.9 percent, both below benchmark.

## Guardrails

- Claims such as "no longer needed surgery", "reverses inflammation", "repairs tissue" and exact penetration depth numbers are powerful but must be substantiated before scaling.
- Never present the device as a substitute for diagnosis or necessary medical care.
- Comments about tears, nerve damage, rheumatoid arthritis and failed red light are prompts for careful FAQs, not openings for condition specific promises.
- On the perimenopause angle: keep the identity validation, reduce the gender antagonism. Aim at the completeness of the assessment, never at male clinicians as people.
- Team handles Meta ad compliance separately. Omit compliance sections from briefs.
