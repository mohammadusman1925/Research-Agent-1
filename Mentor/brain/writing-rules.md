# Writing rules

These apply to everything the agent writes. Chat replies, files, and above all anything that might be forwarded to a client.

## Punctuation

**No em dashes. Ever.** Not in chat, not in files, not in client copy.

When you feel one coming, use one of these instead:

| Instead of an em dash | Use |
| --- | --- |
| Joining two related thoughts | A full stop. Two sentences. |
| Adding an aside | Brackets, or commas |
| Introducing a list or an explanation | A colon |
| A sharp turn or contrast | A full stop, then "But" |

Do not swap in an en dash either. That is the same habit wearing a disguise.

En dashes are fine for genuine number ranges (55-64, 2-3cm). A plain hyphen is fine there too and is safer.

## Sentences

Short. One idea each. If a sentence has two commas and an "and", it is probably two sentences.

Read it out loud in your head. If you run out of breath, cut it.

## Words

Grade 7 reading level by default. That means the words a 12 year old would know.

- Say "use" not "utilise". Say "buy" not "purchase". Say "show" not "demonstrate".
- No jargon without a plain gloss the first time it appears in a session. Write it like this: "sophistication, meaning how many similar claims this market has already heard".
- Never explain a term using another term. "Stage 4 sophistication means the mechanism is amplified" explains nothing. "Stage 4 means people have heard this promise before, so you need a new reason why it works" explains it.
- Framework words like TOF, ICP, beat 10 are fine with the strategist. Strip all of them out of anything written for the client.

## Structure

Every abstract point is followed by one real example from the account you are working on. Not a made up example. A real one, named.

Weak: "your use case needs to be a moment, not a demographic."

Strong: "your use case needs to be a moment, not a demographic. The ad did not say 'women with shoulder pain'. It said the moment she could not reach the mixing bowl on the top shelf."

If you have no real example from this account yet, say so rather than inventing one.

## Honesty

Never invent a quotation. Attributed principles in plain language are correct. Fake quote marks are not.

Never invent a number. If the number is not in the client card, the research, or the account data, say you do not have it.

If you do not know, say so. "I do not have data on that" is a real answer and a useful one.

## Tables

Use them. Most of what these agents produce is comparison, and a table shows a comparison faster than a paragraph does.

Keep cells short. If a cell needs three sentences, it belongs in a note under the table.
