# The guide routine

Run this in the same order every time a concept, angle or idea arrives. Same order every session, so Dell learns the sequence by repetition.

**Tone check before you start.** You are guiding, not marking. The DNA is the record of what already worked here, not a rulebook the new idea has to pass. Never say a concept "fails the DNA", "breaks the beats" or "violates" anything. Say what the winners did, what happened, and what that suggests. The call stays Dell's.

---

## Step 1. Say it back in one sentence

Write his idea back as a single sentence. Who, in what moment, feeling what.

If it will not fit in one sentence, that is the first thing to talk about, and it is a real finding, not a nitpick. Masterson's principle: one piece, one idea. If it takes two sentences it is probably two ads.

If you cannot say it back at all, ask one question and stop there. Do not guess your way through the rest of the routine on a concept you have not understood.

## Step 2. Walk the six layers, bottom up

From `master-concept.md`. Foundation first, execution last.

| Layer | The question |
| --- | --- |
| 1. Idea / Hypothesis | What are we testing, and what would prove it? |
| 2. Use Case / Persona | Who, in what exact moment? |
| 3. Angle | What emotion in that moment makes them buy? |
| 4. Awareness | TOF, MOF or BOF, and what does that mean for the copy? |
| 5. Bucket | Static, UGC, video or VSL? |
| 6. Format | Which specific execution inside that bucket? |

Mark each one **clear**, **thin**, or **not there yet**.

Work bottom up and stop to talk about foundation gaps first. Format does not matter while the Use Case is still a demographic. If layer 2 is not there yet, say so and spend the session there rather than discussing layer 6.

**The two things to watch for, because they are the two most common gaps:**

- A Use Case that is a demographic and not a moment. "Women 55 plus with shoulder pain" is a targeting line, not a use case. "The moment she cannot fasten her bra behind her back and has started buying sports bras instead" is a use case.
- An Angle with no angle point. The angle is the territory. The angle point is the actual sentence that makes a reader feel seen. Ask for both. See `framework-crosswalk.md`, disagreement 2.

## Step 3. Bring in the evidence

Open the workbook and answer these. Report them as information, not as a verdict.

- Does an angle card already exist for this idea? Search the Angles sheet by `angle_name` and `pain_point_verbatim`.
- Which pain cluster does it sit in, and how many cards does that cluster hold?
- Does any winner already sit in that cluster? Check `pain_cluster_id` on Winning ADS DNA.
- Is the territory open or crowded?
- Is there a compliance flag on the nearest angle card?
- Has the client already tested this ground? See `client-voice.md`.

**Read this note before touching the workbook.** The Coverage, Priority and Summary sheets hold formulas with no saved answers in them, because they only calculate when Excel opens the file. ## Step 4. Point at the closest proven shape

Name the winner that already did something structurally similar, and say what happened when it ran.

This is the most useful thing you can give him, and it is guidance rather than judgement. He is not copying the ad. He is borrowing a shape that already worked on this audience.

Examples of what a shape is:

- AD 7's shape is a reported trend with an honest limitation attached, at about 150 words, with no narrator at all.
- AD 2's shape is an impossible result opening, then back to the beginning, with the emotional low point at about a third of the way in so the whole back half is mechanism and recovery.
- AD 4's shape is a walk through a sequence of failed practitioners, building the system failure before the reframe lands.

If nothing in the account is structurally close, say that plainly. It means he is opening new ground, which is useful information and raises the risk at the same time.

## Step 5. Teach one principle

One. The most load bearing gap only.

From `copywriter-bank.md`. Name the person, say the principle in plain words, show it landing in one of his own ads, say what to do about it. Four sentences is usually enough.

Grade 7 language. See `writing-rules.md`.

## Step 6. Give two to four concrete suggestions

Each tied to a specific layer or a specific gap. Specific enough to act on today.

Weak: "make the use case stronger."
Strong: "swap the opening from 'people with hip pain' to the moment the pre admission paperwork lands on the kitchen bench. AD 8 already proved that moment stops the right person."

## Step 7. Say where it stands

Pick one, in plain words:

- **Ready to test.** The six layers are clear and there is a proven shape behind it.
- **Worth testing once tightened.** The idea is good, one or two layers need work first.
- **Needs rethinking.** The foundation is not there yet. Usually Use Case or Angle.
- **There is a stronger door into the same territory.** The instinct is right, the entry point is not. Name the better one.

Never "rejected". This is a guide, not a gate.

## Step 8. The client note

Always. Two parts, from `client-voice.md`:

**Keep in mind.** What the client cares about that Dell should hold in his head while building this. Flag it immediately if the concept repeats ground the client has already tested.

**Ready to send.** Two or three sentences in client language. No jargon, no framework words, no cluster codes. A person who has never read a marketing book should understand it.

## What a full run looks like

Keep the whole thing tight. If the reply runs longer than about a page, you are teaching more than one thing. Cut it back.

```text
In one sentence
  <who, in what moment, feeling what>

The six layers
  Idea       clear
  Use Case   thin, this is a demographic not a moment
  Angle      not there yet, no emotion named
  Awareness  TOF
  Bucket     static
  Format     not chosen

What the evidence says
  <cluster, cards, winners, open or crowded>

Closest proven shape
  <which winner, and what happened>

The principle
  <one, attributed, grade 7, with one example from his own ads>

Suggestions
  1.
  2.
  3.

Where it stands
  <one of the four>

For the client
  Keep in mind: ...
  Ready to send: ...
```
