# Image Rules

*Ported from `Metabolae/05-IMAGE-RULES.md`, 2026-08-08. Built from an audit of 50 creative units, 48 with the image verified. The rules and the hierarchy are brand-agnostic and transfer unchanged; the prompt library and evidence index, which were one brand's, are not included.*

**This file recommends, it does not decide.** Every rule here is what audited winners actually did. A direction that contradicts it needs a reason, but the reason is yours to give. What this guarantees is that no image direction arrives without its precedent named.

---

## The governing rule

> Show the most interesting concrete evidence in the script. If the evidence is invisible, show the person, event, object, setting, proof, or consequence that makes the headline interesting. Preserve the open loop; let the story and mechanism explain the rest.

The image is always a concrete **thing**, never an idea. No text overlays, the headline does that job.

---

## What an image can and cannot do

A relevant native image can improve scroll-stop, click-through, perceived relevance, and the match between headline and reader expectation. It can also reduce negative feedback and policy friction.

**It does not control delivery cost on its own.** Auction competition, audience size, placement, frequency, bid strategy, page quality and downstream conversion feedback all affect delivery. Never promise that an image will lower CPM.

---

## The conflict hierarchy

**This is the decision tool.** When two candidates compete, the higher number wins.

| | Rule | Use when |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Exact referent** | The headline says *this*, *these*, *look*, or *what happened*. Show the exact thing it points at |
| 2 | **Specific visible symptom or event** | A concrete unusual clue exists. It outranks a generic authority portrait |
| 3 | **Proof of the surprising result** | A chart, scan, specimen, record or log is what creates the belief shift |
| 4 | **Authority identity** | The credential is the hook and no stronger visual clue exists |
| 5 | **Product or use** | Correct use, product difference, or a warm high-intent decision is central |
| 6 | **Lifestyle outcome** | Only when no stronger concrete clue or proof exists |

**The most common mistake is defaulting to level 4.** In the audit, several Authority-concept winners used a level 2 or 3 image and beat the portrait. Concept type suggests a starting point; the hierarchy decides.

---

## Operating sequence

1. **Finish the script and headline first.** A changed headline voids its image direction.
2. Summarize the story in one sentence.
3. State the mechanism separately. Do not force the image to explain it.
4. Underline the headline's most concrete symptom, identity, event, object, setting, proof, contradiction or consequence.
5. Apply the conflict hierarchy. Select **one** visual role.
6. Give the image one focal subject. Preserve the open loop.
7. Match product visibility to awareness.
8. Create one primary, three controlled backups, one safer low-shock control.
9. Score before production. Test one major variable at a time.

## What the winners actually did

| Pattern family | Units | Share |
|---|---|---|
| Symptom / consequence / diagnostic proof | 16 | 32% |
| Authority identity or authority-proof hybrid | 11 | 22% |
| Event, setting, history, or community world | 8 | 16% |
| Product contrast, failed method, documentary evidence | 7 | 14% |
| Avatar, product-use, measurement, desired outcome | 6 | 12% |
| Source unavailable, strategy only | 2 | 4% |

**Read this as a corrective.** Nearly a third of winners showed a symptom or consequence, not a person. Only a fifth led with authority identity. The instinct to put an expert's face on everything is not what the data shows.

## Product visibility by funnel

| Stage | Product |
|---|---|
| Cold TOF | Usually hidden. The image sets up the problem, not the answer |
| MOF | Natural, in hand, in routine, secondary to the avatar |
| BOF | Specification-led. Product and proof can lead |

**Never show the product before the mechanism appears in the copy.**

## Concept defaults

**Start here, then check the hierarchy.** If the hierarchy points elsewhere, the hierarchy wins.

| Concept | Default | Show |
|---|---|---|
| **Authority** | The persona | The expert in their real work setting, at camera. If the hook names a specific threat, show that instead. The persona's identity *is* the image |
| **Testimonial** | The problem state | The failed ritual, the bad reading, the stuck number. Never the solution, always what went wrong *before* the discovery |
| **Curiosity** | The mystery object | The symptom or thing that makes no sense. The viewer must think "wait, what?" before reading |
| **Storytelling** | The opening scene | The historical setting, unexpected character, dramatic location. Transport the viewer somewhere unexpected |
| **Anti-** | The system being challenged | The persona defiant at camera, or the artifact that failed them. Confrontation energy |
| **Education** | The symptom | Close-up of the thing being explained. The viewer must recognize their own body. Not the educator |
| **Caregiver** | The couple together | Caregiver and patient side by side. The relationship *is* the hook. Never just one person |
| **Sale** | The product | Clean, in context. Differentiation-led hooks show a side-by-side contrast |
| **Us vs Them** | The "them" side | The pile of failed alternatives. The mess of everything that doesn't work *is* the image |

## Reusable rules

Grouped by what triggers them. Each was extracted from a verified winner.

**The headline names something literal**
- Says "this" or "these", show the exact referent. Headline and image are incomplete without each other
- Names a visible, unusual symptom, show that exact symptom in one unmistakable frame
- Names a location or setting, show the exact setting as a personal snapshot. Let the copy reveal what happened there
- Names a day count, show the calendar-adjacent object: the bottle, the tracker, the counter
- Reclassifies a surface symptom ("X is not a Y problem"), show either the symptom or the hidden cause, whichever is clearer and less graphic
- "The dangerous sign is not X", show the true revealed sign. Authority identity is the fallback only

**Proof, records and results**
- The turning point is a recorded result, show the record, screen, chart or note. Not a reaction shot
- The hook centers on a measurement or decline, show the corresponding evidence, but only if it reads in one glance
- A technical term is unfamiliar, translate it into a recognizable consequence. Avoid melodrama; it undermines trust
- Exposé or anti-conventional copy, show the evidence that forced the belief change, not the narrator's emotion
- Blunt contrarian hooks, credible evidence beats an equally aggressive reaction image

**Authority**
- "What I would do as an expert", the expert's identity in their real work setting. The environment proves the role without a text badge
- Authority and symptom equally important, combine the expert identity with one clean diagnostic object
- A concrete clue is the stronger scroll-stopper, let it outrank the portrait, even inside an Authority script
- A testimonial narrator's profession materially increases belief, show the identity cue before the product
- Expertise is institutional rather than personal, show the team, lab, or research environment
- Demographic-specific authority, show the represented community respectfully, in a natural setting

**Failed method**
- Failed-method stories, show the moment of rejection, disposal or discovery. Not a generic before/after pose
- "The old way failed", show the mess, residue, unused item or discarded routine that proves the effort was real
- Long failed-effort testimonials, show the emotional or lifestyle cost of failure when that cost is more relatable than the product

**Story worlds**
- Story ads, show the most dramatic first scene. Never reveal the resolution in the image
- Historical hooks, pair the character or event with the unusual object driving the mystery
- Ancient-secret stories, the era plus one artifact that makes the knowledge feel recorded rather than mythical
- Community-origin stories, real people and place cues, with dignity. Never reduce a group to a costume or stereotype

**Testimonial and caregiver**
- Emotional testimonial, the exact avatar at the story's lowest point or turning point
- First-person testimonial, prioritize avatar recognition and believable ownership over polished product photography
- Timed transformation, combine the avatar, the intervention, and one believable proof cue
- Caregiver copy, the observer, expression matching the story's concern and relief, plus one concrete sign of the struggle
- Outcome images, only when the avatar's desired life is more motivating than the failed method, and keep the headline link explicit

**Consequence and risk**
- A turning point, the consequence scene or the person living it. Not an abstract number
- "Avoid this outcome" copy, one recognizable consequence, in the least graphic version that still communicates the stakes
- Consequence images generally, only when genuinely central. Otherwise authority identity is clearer and safer

**Funnel and iteration**
- At BOF, product and specification clarity may outrank native mystery, but the comparison must stay accurate
- Stage-based education, expert plus one consequence or diagnostic object. Never a crowded infographic at TOF
- **Iterating a winner**, preserve the visual role and the belief shift. Change the object, setting or proof cue. Never change the core contradiction

## Production defaults

```
1:1 aspect ratio
Photorealistic
Shot on an older camera -- early digital or film. Slight grain, mild noise,
imperfect focus, natural white balance. No modern phone HDR, no commercial
gloss, no over-sharpening.
One clear focal subject
Looks like believable content, not a polished display ad

NEVER: overlay text, watermarks, logos, copied faces, stock-photo smiles,
split screens, crowded benefit stacks, gore, readable private data,
medication-replacement implications, guaranteed-outcome imagery
```

## Prompt skeleton

```
VISUAL ROLE:        [from the conflict hierarchy]
VISUAL MAGNET:      [the single most interesting concrete thing]
SCENE:              [what is happening]
SUBJECT:            [who or what, with age, role, appearance]
SETTING:            [room, time of day, light quality]
PROOF OBJECT:       [object, or none]
PRODUCT VISIBILITY: [none / secondary / primary] and why
EMOTION:            [one word]
CAMERA:             [see production defaults]
OPEN LOOP:          [what stays unexplained]
```

Specificity is the whole job. Age, room, time of day, light quality, what is worn, what is on the surface. Generic prompts produce stock images, and stock images do not stop scroll.

## Preflight scorecard

Score before production. **0-5 each**, note this scale differs from the 1-5 copy scorecard. Never average the two.

**Strengths**
1. Headline-image congruence
2. Curiosity / open-loop strength
3. Native credibility
4. Single-focal clarity
5. Avatar / market recognition
6. Funnel and product fit

**Penalties**
7. Shock or negative-feedback risk
8. Medical or personal-attribute policy risk
9. Copyright or identity risk
10. Ad-like or stock-photo appearance

**Then answer**
- What will someone understand in one second?
- Is the most interesting part of the script visible?
- Is authority identity being used only when it is genuinely the visual magnet?
- Does the image close the curiosity loop too early?
- What safer control should be tested alongside it?
- Keep, revise, or reject?

---

## Iteration axes

Change one major variable per test so results stay interpretable: subject · setting or context · camera distance and crop · proof object · emotion · product visibility · lighting · time of day.

## Sourcing the image from the hook

**The image comes from whatever the hook names.** Not from the use case as a category, not from the pain point as a category. From the hook. That is why the operating sequence says finish the headline first: a changed headline voids its image direction.

### What the hook names, and where the image comes from

| If the hook names | The image comes from | Beat |
|---|---|---|
| The situation they are in | The use case | 1 and 6 |
| A thing they can no longer do | The pain point | 4 |
| Why the last treatment failed | The explanation | 10 |
| A number or a result | The proof | 14 |
| The offer or the price | The product | 13 |

### The gate that overrules it

Awareness. If the two disagree, awareness wins.

| Awareness | Image comes from | Never show |
|---|---|---|
| Problem aware, cold | The use case or the pain point | The product. It has not been earned |
| Solution aware | The explanation. Diagram, hidden cause, depth | Lifestyle shots |
| Most aware | The product and the offer | A symptom. They are past that |

### The pattern, four steps

1. Read the hook.
2. Circle every noun you could photograph.
3. If there is more than one, take the most specific and the least expected.
4. That noun is the image, alone in the frame, nothing else.

### When the hook has no photographable noun

Go up one step, to the scene the hook came from, and take an object out of that.

**Never invent an object that is not in the copy.** If it is not in the script, it does not go in the frame.

### Two tests before you shoot

**The swap test.** Could this image sit on any other ad in the category? If yes, it is not specific enough. Go back to step 3.

**The loop test.** Does the image answer the question or ask it? It has to ask. An image that gives away the ending removes the reason to read.
