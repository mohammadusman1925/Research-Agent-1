# UGC brief template

**This is the shape every UGC creator brief follows.** It is reusable across clients. The structure is the rule. The words inside it are client facts and live in `clients/<brand>.md`.

Source: four UGC creator briefs written for one client, August 2026. The four concepts they covered were surgery avoidance, tried everything, social discovery, and an untapped male segment.

---

## The one idea behind it

**A creator brief gives direction and permission, not a script.**

Talking points, not lines. Three hook options, not one. "Pick what feels natural and skip anything that doesn't." A creator who is handed a script reads it, and it sounds read. A creator who is handed the situation and the feeling tells it, and it sounds true.

The strategy stays in the tracker. Never put beats, ICP codes, awareness stages or sophistication in a creator brief. They do not know the words and they do not need them.

## The sections, in order

### 1. Header

| Line | What it holds |
| --- | --- |
| Brand name | Small, above everything |
| `UGC Creator Briefs, <product>` | The set this belongs to |
| `Brief N: <short name>` | Which one of the set |
| Creator tag | `FEMALE CREATOR` or `MALE CREATOR` |
| Title, in quote marks | The concept in the creator's own words, not the strategy name |
| One line under it | Why this brief exists. "The brand's top performing concept by volume" |

### 2. Hey, here is what you need to know

The product in plain words, in about three sentences. Say what it is not, first: not a heat pack, not a TENS machine. Then the four things it does.

Then a strip of three facts: **price, guarantee, shipping.**

### 3. Your story direction

The concept, then why it connects, then one line that hands it over.

> You are that person. Or you were.

Ends by naming what the video is not. "This isn't a product review. It's a real moment."

### 4. The key idea

The mechanism, in a quote box, in the plainest possible words. Then the release:

> You don't need to say it like that. Just let this idea come through naturally.

Followed by the one sentence the viewer should walk away thinking.

### 5. Who's watching

The ICP written as a person, not a targeting line. Age, country, what they have already tried, what they no longer believe.

**Always end this section with when they are watching.** "She's watching your video at 11pm because she can't sleep from the pain." That single line does more for the tone than everything above it.

### 6. Things you might talk about

Bullets. Ten to fifteen. A mix of story beats, product facts, and outcomes.

They are options, not a running order. The heading says "might" on purpose.

### 7. Ad inspiration

One link to examples of the style. Nothing else.

### 8. How to film this

Opens with **Tone**, written as a comparison a person can feel. "Like talking to a friend over tea who has just told you they have been offered surgery."

Then timestamped blocks. Five is the right number:

| Block | Seconds | Holds |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hook | 0-8 or 0-10 | Three alternative openings, written out |
| The story | 8-35 | What happened. Prompts to be specific |
| Show the product | 35-55 | Physical instructions. Strap it on, dim the room |
| The shift | 55-75 | What changed. Push for honest timelines |
| Close | 75-90 | The sign off, guarantee, link |

Give the hook block **three options**. One is an instruction. Three is a choice, and the creator picks the one they can say without flinching.

### 9. Emotional flow

Five words in a chain, showing the journey. Then one line naming the register.

> Fear of surgery, tired of trying, sceptical hope, quiet surprise, relief.
> This is not an excitement video. It's a relief video. Heavy to light.

That last line is the most useful sentence in the whole brief. It stops a creator performing.

### 10. Technical specs

Format, resolution, fps, length, subtitles, music, filters, watermarks.

**Say what not to do.** Do not add subtitles. Do not add music. No filters. We handle it.

### 11. What to send us

| Item | Spec |
| --- | --- |
| Main video | 1x vertical, 60-90 sec |
| Short cut | 1x vertical, 15-30 sec |
| Raw footage | All unedited files |
| Photos | 2-3 natural shots |

### 12. B-roll and extra footage

The half of the brief that keeps paying months later. Two parts.

**Quick filming tips.** Separate clips, 5 to 15 seconds each, steady, natural light, either orientation, no talking needed, multiple angles.

**Then seven or eight named categories**, each with a clip count and a one line note underneath explaining the thinking:

| Category | Clips | Always in |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Unboxing and first impressions | 6-8 | Yes |
| Product close ups and details | 5-7 | Yes |
| Putting it on | 4-6 | Yes |
| Wearing it, daily life | 8-10 | Yes |
| Movement, the after moments | 8-10 | Yes |
| Pain, the before feel | 5-7 | Yes |
| Lifestyle and environment | 4-6 | Yes |
| One category built from the concept | 5-8 | Varies |

That last one is what makes each brief different. The Tried Everything brief has "The tried everything shots": the medicine drawer, the heat pack unused on a shelf, closing the drawer. The Social Discovery brief has "Show and tell shots": holding it up like showing a friend.

**Build that category out of the concept's own imagery.** If the story mentions a drawer, ask for the drawer.

The notes under each category carry the craft:

- "A 5 second shot of a drawer full of creams and pills is worth more than 30 seconds of talking. Show, don't tell."
- "Don't perform these, just do them. The power is in how ordinary they look."
- "Move the way you used to. Slowly, carefully, protectively. We use these for before and after contrast."
- "The more this is just my Tuesday it looks, the better."

### 13. Raw footage

Ask for the outtakes, the false starts, the takes they almost deleted. Say why: the best moments come from those.

### 14. Links, payment, confidentiality

Landing page, product page, payment, duration, and one line asking them not to share the brief.

## How many briefs in a set

Four is the right number, and they cover **four different doorways into the same product**, not four versions of one story.

| Doorway | Who it reaches |
| --- | --- |
| The decision they cannot undo | Facing surgery or a procedure |
| Tried everything | Exhausted, out of trust |
| Social discovery | Not looking. Scrolling. Finds it |
| The one nobody has made yet | The segment with zero creative |

That fourth slot is the most valuable one in any account. Fill it with whoever is being ignored.

---

## Guardrails, every time

- **Never put an unsourced number in a creator's mouth.** A creator saying a statistic on camera is a claim the brand made.
- **Frame results as one person's experience.** "I noticed a difference after a few sessions" is safe. "This works" is not.
- **Push for honest timelines.** "It wasn't overnight, but after two weeks" outperforms a miracle and survives review.
- **If a brief labels a concept as best or highest performing, check the figure is settled** before it goes out. A disputed number becomes a claim once it is written down.
- **Never invent a creator's story for them.** Give the situation and the feeling. They supply the truth.
