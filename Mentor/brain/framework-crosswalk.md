# Framework crosswalk

Dell runs two systems that describe the same work in different words. Module 1.1 (the Master Concept) and the Ad-DNA system. If the mentor mixes the vocabularies without translating, it will contradict itself.

This file is the translation table and the record of where the two genuinely disagree.

### Awareness, the fuller map

Module 1.1 uses three stages. The Ad-DNA system uses Schwartz's five. Three does not divide into five cleanly, so hold both:

| Schwartz stage | Module 1.1 stage | What it means in one line | Our example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Unaware | TOF | Does not know they have the problem | none in the account, this is a gap |
| Problem Aware | TOF | Feels the problem, does not know a fix exists | AD 1, AD 2, AD 4 |
| Solution Aware | MOF | Knows fixes exist, does not know us | AD 5, AD 7, AD 8 |
| Product Aware | MOF | Knows us, not sure we fit them | none in the account, this is a gap |
| Most Aware | BOF | Ready, just needs a reason to act now | AD 6 |

Two of the five stages have no creative at all. That is worth saying out loud when the subject comes up.

## Where the two systems genuinely disagree

Do not smooth these over. Say both, then say which one you are trusting here and why.

### 1. The 70/20/10 split versus what this account actually did

Module 1.1 sets a default of TOF 70 percent, MOF 20 percent, BOF 10 percent of output.

This account's results run the other way on efficiency:

- The highest volume ad is AD 7, the Quiet Pattern. 39 purchases. It is MOF.
- The highest ROAS ad is AD 6, the EOFY sale. 6x. It is BOF, and it has no story at all.
- The long form TOF story ads sit at 3x to 4x.

**How to handle it.** The module's split is about output volume, not about which single ad wins. Both things are true at once. TOF is what keeps new people entering, and if it stops, MOF and BOF starve. But this account has proof that its short MOF and BOF ads convert efficiently, and that is a real finding worth acting on.

So: keep TOF as the majority of new production, because that is what feeds the machine. But do not let anyone conclude that long form storytelling is the only thing that works here. The evidence says the funnel works as a system, not as individual persuasion units.

### 2. Angle is one thing in Module 1.1, two things in the DNA read

Module 1.1: an Angle is "the emotional reason that situation makes them buy."

The DNA read splits it:

- `angle` is the territory. For example "surgery wait reversal".
- `angle_point` is the actual line that carries it. For AD 2 that is "That's the joint. That's not why you can't sleep on your side. That's not why it stabs the second you stand up."

**How to handle it.** Ask for both. An angle with no angle point is a topic, not an angle. The angle point is the sentence that makes a reader feel the ad was written about them, and it is usually the thing that is missing when a concept feels vague.

### 3. The diversification rule cannot be run here yet

Module 1.1: when a Use Case times Angle wins in one bucket, run it across all four before moving to a new concept. Exhaustion is per bucket, not per angle.

This account has only ever tested one bucket. Every single winner is a static native text ad. Video exists but underperforms: hook rate 46.1 percent, hold rate 32.9 percent. Image statics carry 87.1 percent of purchases.

**How to handle it.** This is the single biggest structural gap in the account, and it is a gap in the module's own terms. There are proven Use Case times Angle pairs sitting in one bucket that have never been run in the other three. AD 2 has never been a UGC. AD 7 has never been a VSL. The rule says that is free upside.

Do not present this as "you are breaking the rule". Present it as "the module says a winner should be run four ways, and you have four winners that have only been run one way."

### 4. Sophistication has no home in Module 1.1

Module 1.1 does not mention Schwartz's sophistication stages, meaning how many similar claims this market has already heard. The Ad-DNA system leans on it heavily. This account sits at Stage 4 going on 5.

**How to handle it.** Awareness and sophistication are different questions. Awareness is "what does this person know". Sophistication is "what have they already been promised by everyone else". A TOF ad in a Stage 4 market still needs a new mechanism, because the audience has heard the plain promise before. That is why the wrong depth and wrong tissue argument carries every winner here.

## Quick answers the mentor should have ready

**"Is this TOF or MOF?"**
Give both vocabularies. "That is MOF in the module's language, Solution Aware in ours. AD 7 and AD 8 both sit there, and AD 7 is the highest volume ad in the account."

**"What bucket should this be?"**
Answer honestly: everything proven here is static native text. Anything else is a genuine test, not a safe bet. Say that clearly rather than pretending video is proven.

**"What format code is this?"**
The 72 codes are the Ad-DNA system's. In this account only three have ever run: #46 long form text, #45 static native, #57 flash sale. Module 1.1's format list (Before/After, Listicle, Pain Highlight and so on) is a different naming layer that sits underneath the bucket. Both can be named at once.
