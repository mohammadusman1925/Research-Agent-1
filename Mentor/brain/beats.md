# The 19 Beats, in four phases

The structural spine of a long-form direct-response script. Target 1,400-2,200 words. Authority and Education concepts may run to 2,800. Sale runs short at 800-1,200.

**Not every beat is in every script.** A missing "Always" beat is a structural fault. A missing "Sometimes" beat is a choice. Most working scripts run 13 to 16 of the 19.

---

## The four phases

Every beat belongs to one. The phase tells you what that stretch of the ad is for.

| Phase | Beats | Its job |
|---|---|---|
| **Phase 1** | 1 to 4 | Stop them and make them recognise themselves |
| **Phase 2** | 5 to 9 | Break the old explanation |
| **Phase 3** | 10 to 13 | Give them the new one, then the product |
| **Phase 4** | 14 to 19 | Prove it and close |

---

## Number order is not reading order

A script can place a beat out of numerical sequence on purpose. The most common one: the **guarantee at beat 18 delivered early**, inside the story, as a line the narrator says, before the results at beat 14.

That is deliberate and it usually makes the ad stronger, because a guarantee spoken by a person reads as a reason they took the risk, not as a footer.

So when you map a script, map it in **reading order** and note the beat numbers. Do not reorder the script to match the numbers.

## What each beat does

### Beat 1, Hook
Stop the right person. Eight opening shapes, keyed to concept type. Templates in `LIST-2-HOOKS-AND-CONCEPTS.md`.

### Beat 2, Credential
Why this narrator gets to speak. Three registers: **authority** (the years and the volume), **peer** (I am one of you), **refusal** (I am nobody, and that is the point). The third is the strongest when the market is sick of experts.

### Beat 3, Personal Crisis / Emotional Setup
The narrator's own stake. Three shapes: their own diagnosis or event; a family member's decline as the preview of their own future; or naked fear, in a specific physical scene, the car park, the 3am kitchen.

### Beat 4, Pattern / Systemic Problem
Zoom out. This is not one person's bad luck, it is a sequence that happens to everybody, in the same order, and nobody names it. Either **the cascade** (this, then this, then this) or **the statistic** that reframes.

### Beat 5, Lies / Myths *(sometimes)*
Numbered. Usually three. Each one is something the reader currently believes, stated fairly, then dismantled. The Authority concept's signature beat.

### Beat 6, Failed Attempts
Everything they already tried. Named specifically, with durations, the list is the proof of effort. Include the near-miss: the thing that *should* have worked and did not, because that is what the mechanism will later explain.

### Beat 7, Emotional Low Point *(usually)*
Not a summary of feeling. One concrete scene where the cost shows. The strongest versions name what was given up rather than what was suffered.

### Beat 8, Bridge / Discovery
Short. Fifteen to forty words. The pivot. Something arrives from outside, a person, a paper, an object on a desk.

### Beat 9, Patient Encounter / External Proof
The first proof that is not the narrator's own opinion. A third party whose result should not have happened and did.

### Beat 10, Mechanism
The heart. *How* it works, not that it works. One analogy, carried the whole way, never mixed. The strongest mechanisms follow one of four shapes: **a blocked pathway**, **a stuck switch**, **accumulating damage**, or **a missing input**. Name the shape before writing it.

### Beat 11, Cheap Version Fails
Pre-empts the obvious objection: *I can get that anywhere*. Same word on the label, different thing in the bottle.

### Beat 12, Differentiation
Why the alternatives fail. Most brands lock a fixed triad here and **never reorder it**, because the order is an argument, each failure earns the next. The brand's triad is in `01-BRAND-SETUP.md`.

### Beat 13, Product Introduction
The full spec, plainly. This is where the brand name first appears, and never earlier.

### Beat 14, Personal Results (Day-by-Day)
The diary. Dated or weeked. The rule that makes it believable: **week one, nothing.** Then a small physical change noticed by someone else before the narrator notices it. Then the measurement, last.

### Beat 15, Expert Validation
A professional reacts to the result. They endorse the *result*, never the product, that distinction is both a compliance line and the reason it is believable.

### Beat 16, Proof, Others
Volume. Several people, one sentence each. Best when it undersells: *"That is not a study. But it is not nothing either."*

### Beat 17, CTA
Two registers. **Direct**, go look at the thing in your house right now, then do this. **Soft**, I am not telling you what to do, I am telling you what I did. Soft outperforms at cold traffic.

### Beat 18, Guarantee
The risk reversal, stated in the reader's terms, not the merchant's.

### Beat 19, Echo Close
Return to the hook's image or the core message, one line, no new information. Authority narrators may sign off: `~ [NAME], [CREDENTIAL], [YEARS] years`.
