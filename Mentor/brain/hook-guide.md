# The Hook Inspiration Guide

Source: `Hook_Inspiration_Guide_Dell.pdf`, Meta Ads Mastery. 30 numbered frameworks across 5 categories, plus 25 starter phrases.

**What it is for.** The first 3 to 5 seconds of a video ad. It is a video and UGC toolkit, not a long form copy toolkit. Read the warning section before applying any of it to this account.

---

## The five categories

- **A. Curiosity gap and pattern interrupts.** Open a loop the viewer has to close.
- **B. Pain agitation and problem solving.** Name and amplify a specific pain.
- **C. Logic, specificity and reason why.** Data, comparisons and mechanisms. Specificity implies truth.
- **D. Social proof and authority.** We look to others to decide what is correct.
- **E. Urgency, scarcity and utility.** Fear of missing out, or a fast win.

## The 25 starters

**Curiosity.** "Nobody talks about this but..." / "Here's what they don't tell you about..." / "The truth about [topic] that no one admits..." / "You're not supposed to know this but..." / "Delete this before [authority] sees it..."

**Pattern interrupt.** "Okay hear me out..." / "This is going to sound crazy but..." / "I know this looks weird but..." / "Before you keep scrolling..." / "As crazy as this sounds..."

**Social proof.** "Everyone's asking me about..." / "This is blowing up right now..." / "I keep getting DMs about..." / "My followers made me post this..." / "This video got [X] views so I'm reposting..."

**Personal story.** "Something happened to me last week..." / "I've never told anyone this but..." / "I learned this the hard way..." / "I wish someone told me this earlier..." / "The biggest mistake I ever made was..."

**Direct challenge.** "Stop doing [X] immediately..." / "You're making this mistake right now..." / "This is why you're not getting results..." / "If you're still doing [X], watch this..." / "Hot take: [controversial opinion]..."

Note that "I wish someone told me this earlier" is almost word for word the sign off already used in the account's best long form ads: "I wish someone had told me eighteen months ago. So now I'm telling you." The instinct is already proven here.

## Read this before using any of it

Three things make a straight import wrong for this account.

### 1. This is a video toolkit. Every winner here is static text.

The guide is built for the first 3 to 5 seconds of a video. This account has never won with video: hook rate 46.1 percent and hold rate 32.9 percent, both under benchmark, while image statics carry 87.1 percent of purchases.

So these frameworks are not proven on any one account until that account runs them. They are proven somewhere. That still makes them useful, and it makes them the right tool the moment a client starts producing UGC or video. Just never present one as a safe bet on an account that has only ever won with statics.

### 2. Do not let it become a third hook vocabulary

The account already has the locked 9 hook styles in `LIST-2-HOOKS-AND-CONCEPTS.md`, and every decoded winner is tagged with one. Adding 30 more names without a bridge would make the workbook unreadable.

Always name the locked style as well as the framework. The map:

| Guide framework | Locked hook style |
| --- | --- |
| 1 Insider Secret, 5 Prediction | Historical, or Confrontation |
| 2 Stop Command, 6 Contrarian Take | Confrontation |
| 3 Weird Mechanism, 15 Mechanism Reveal | Symptom Reframe |
| 4 Qualifier, 11 Symptom Call-Out, 12 Relatable Frustration | Empathy + Confrontation |
| 7 Silent Killer | Symptom Reframe, or Negation Chain |
| 8 Desperation Story, 9 Mistake Listicle | Failed Quest |
| 10 Magic Button, 26 Quick Tips | Promise territory, no locked style fits cleanly |
| 13 Us vs Them, 18 Value Stack | Negation Chain |
| 14 Statistical Authority, 24 Expert Endorsement | Credential Lead |
| 16 Demonstration, 29 Before/After, 30 Visual Metaphor | visual, not a hook style. Tag the image role instead |
| 17 Specific Number, 20 Skeptic Convert, 22 Review Highlight | Impossible Result |
| 19 Viral Trend | Confrontation, the social trend variant |
| 21 Founder Story | Credential Lead |
| 23 Unboxing, 25 Scarcity, 27 Risk Reversal, 28 Exclusive Invitation | offer mechanics, not hook styles |

If a framework has no locked equivalent, say so and flag it as a candidate `[NEW: ...]` for the list. Never quietly invent a tenth hook style.

### 3. Some of these are unsafe for a health product in Australia

Read the guardrails in the client card before using any of these. For a health adjacent product, or any category with a regulator, four frameworks are genuinely risky.

| Framework | The problem |
| --- | --- |
| **14 Statistical Authority** | "According to [authority], [X]% of people..." invites a number nobody can source. The account's own guardrails require every claim to be substantiated. Only use it with a real, citable figure from the research. |
| **24 Expert Endorsement** | "I'm a [credential] and this is the only [product] I recommend." Module 1.1 is explicit that the authority must be real and that Meta prohibits implied medical credibility without substantiation. If the client card names an expert who is an advertorial persona rather than a real endorser, they must stay disclosed as one. |
| **1 Insider Secret** | "The secret doctors don't want you to know" is the classic health claim shape that gets accounts restricted. The account already makes this argument in a safer form: the scan was looking in the wrong place. Use that version. |
| **20 Skeptic Convert** | "I thought red light therapy was BS until I tried it" is the guide's own example, and it fits this product perfectly. But it edges into an efficacy claim. Frame it as one person's experience with "individual results may vary", never as a general result. |

Everything else in the guide is fine to work with, subject to the normal compliance flag on the angle card.

## The five frameworks worth reaching for first

Not because they are best in general. Because each one closes a gap this account actually has.

**#11 The Symptom Call-Out.** "Do you suffer from [symptom 1], [symptom 2], or [symptom 3]?" This is the 2AM Test brief from the Core Memory, already written and never built. PC04 Sleep Disruption holds 11 angle cards and no winners. Symptoms are already in the review corpus, verbatim: waking whenever she rolls onto it, two hours a night, cannot fasten a bra.

**#27 The Risk Reversal.** Four of the account's short ads carry no guarantee, and the two that do are the two most complete. Kennedy's principle in a hook shape. There is a 30 day money back promise sitting unused.

**#13 The Us vs Them Comparison.** This is the Why Heat Fades brief. Heat pack versus wearable, surface warmth versus combined modalities. It answers the single most common objection from people who already own a heat pack, and it makes the depth argument visual.

**#16 The Demonstration and #30 The Visual Metaphor.** Every ad in this account is text on a plain background. These two are the direct answer to that gap. The copy is already handing over the visuals: the sock, the paperwork on the kitchen bench, the top shelf, the bra strap.

**#20 The Skeptic Convert.** Wendy said in the comments that red light did nothing for her knee or shoulder. That is the core efficacy objection in the account. This framework answers it head on with a real person, which is exactly what UGC is for. Handle with the compliance note above.

## How to use it in a session

When Dell is briefing a video, VSL or UGC asset, offer two or three frameworks, not a list of thirty. Pick them by the gap, not by the category.

For each one, give:

1. The framework number and name
2. The locked hook style it maps to
3. A version written in this account's own customer language, not the guide's generic example
4. Any compliance note from the table above

The guide's own instruction is the right one: change the specifics, keep the psychological structure intact. Its generic examples are for other categories. The words have to come from `Customer-Language-Bank.md` and the review corpus, or the hook will sound like every other health ad.
