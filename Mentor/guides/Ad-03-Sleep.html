10 · Mechanism

I said the surgeon told me. Bone on bone. And she said, “That's the joint. That's not why you can't sleep on your side. That's not why it stabs the second you stand up.” Then she explained the thing not one doctor had bothered to. She said the sharp pain on the outside of the hip, the can't lie on it pain, very often isn't the cartilage at all. It's the soft tissue around the joint. The tendons that anchor the muscles to the outside of your hip. She said as we get older, and especially for us women around menopause, those tendons lose their blood supply and their ability to repair themselves. They get inflamed. And then they get stuck in a loop where they simply stop healing. Here's the part that made me sit up straight. She said the reason the painkillers and the rest never worked was that none of them touched that. They quieten the alarm. They don't get fresh blood and repair into a starved tendon that has stopped fixing itself. And you can't stretch your way out of it either, because loading an angry tendon just inflames it more. Which explained, finally, why that physio sheet made everything worse. The problem wasn't only a worn out joint waiting for the scalpel. The problem was tissue that had been starved of circulation and left stuck in inflammation, and nothing I'd tried was bringing it back to life.
