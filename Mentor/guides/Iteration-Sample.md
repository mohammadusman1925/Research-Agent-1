**Control hook**

The 14 month wait for my hip replacement turned out to be the best thing that ever happened to me, because by my surgery date I no longer needed it.

I still haven't told my surgeon that.

**Door A**

I sat on the edge of my bed for eleven minutes one morning, holding a sock.

Sixty four years old, and I could not reach my own foot.

That was the morning I stopped telling myself it would settle down on its own.

### Why Opens on an object in one moment, with no story built first. Same shape as the strongest opening in the account.    Risk The sock is proven as a payoff 700 words in. It is unproven as a cold opening. That gap is the whole point of the test.

**Control hook**

**Control hook**

The 14 month wait for my hip replacement turned out to be the best thing that ever happened to me, because by my surgery date I no longer needed it.

I still haven't told my surgeon that.

I'd sit in the dark kitchen with a heat pack on my hip, waiting for the sky to go grey.

### Why Opens on night pain, the single biggest theme in the review corpus, and the one no ad has ever led with.    Risk Furthest from the control, so it is the highest variance door. Sleep has 11 angle cards and zero winners behind it.

## The image one per door

Every image in this account so far is text on a plain background. Zero symptom images have ever run, despite the copy handing over the sock, the letter, the heat pack. So each direction below is untested here. One image per script, no backups, no options. The method's own rule: show the most interesting concrete thing in the script. Never an idea, never a text overlay, and on cold traffic never the product, because the belt has not been earned yet.

The rule. The image comes from whatever the hook names. Not from the use case as a category, not from the pain point as a category. From the hook. That is why the method says finish the headline first: a changed headline voids its image direction.

| If the hook names | The image comes from | Beat |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The situation she is in | The use case | 1 and 6 |
| A thing she can no longer do | The pain point | 4 |
| Why the last treatment failed | The explanation | 10 |
| A number or a result | The proof | 14 |
| The offer or the price | The product | 13 |

The gate that overrules it. Awareness. If the two disagree, awareness wins. Problem aware and cold: the use case or the pain point, and never the product because it has not been earned. Solution aware: the explanation, the diagram, the depth. Most aware: the product and the offer, never a symptom. Every script on this page is problem aware, so the belt never appears.

The pattern, four steps. 1. Read the hook. 2. Circle every noun you could photograph. 3. If there is more than one, take the most specific and the least expected. 4. That noun is the image, alone in the frame, nothing else.

| Hook | The noun it sticks to | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| “I was on a waiting list. Fourteen months.” | the waiting list | Use case |
| “I had two injections left in that hip.” | the injections | Use case |
| “Eleven minutes, holding a sock.” | the sock | Pain point |
| “Looked at my scan for about ninety seconds.” | the scan | Use case |
| “Sat in the dark kitchen with a heat pack.” | the heat pack | Pain point |

Two tests before you shoot. The swap test: could this image sit on any other ad in the category? If yes, it is not specific enough, go back to step 3. The loop test: does the image answer the question or ask it? It has to ask. An image that gives away the ending kills the reason to read.

Run the image test before the copy tests. Same winning ad, same headline, text card against the concrete photo. That is one clean variable and it answers the biggest open question in the account. Then run the copy tests on whichever image style won. If you change the image and the copy in the same round, you will not know which one moved the number.

- Move one beat. If a second beat moved, you are not iterating, you are writing a new concept.
- Keep the bridge line into the body identical across every door. That is the seam, and it has to hold.
- Do not touch the echo close. This one is generic enough to survive all three openings, so leave it alone.
- Run all three doors at once against the control. Hooks are the cheapest thing you will ever test.
- Name them ITE and bump the version. Same angle code, same batch. Only the version moves.
- Judge on click through first, then CPA. A door can win attention and still lose the sale.
