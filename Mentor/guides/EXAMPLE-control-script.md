# EXAMPLE ONLY, another brand's ad

**This is an illustration, not evidence.** It belongs to a hip belt brand and it is here so the agent has something concrete to point at when explaining a move.

Never quote it as if it were the current client's work. Never cite its numbers as the current client's numbers. When the current client has a decoded winner of their own, replace this file.

## The script

**Beat 1, Hook**

The 14 month wait for my hip replacement turned out to be the best thing that ever happened to me, because by my surgery date I no longer needed it.

I still haven't told my surgeon that.

But if you're reading this with that deep ache in the side of your hip right now, you need to hear how I got here.

**Beat 2, Credential**

My name is Maggie.

I'm 64.

And eighteen months ago I honestly believed my life as I knew it was over.

**Beat 3, Personal Crisis**

It didn't start with a fall or an accident.

Just a dull soreness on the outside of my right hip that I was sure would go away on its own.

It didn't.

One morning I went to step off the kerb outside my house and my hip just gave out.

The pain jumped from 0 to 9 in a second.

I grabbed the fence so hard I scraped my palm, standing there in my dressing gown at 6am, thinking, what is happening to me.

After that day, the pain never really left.

It moved into my life like it owned the place.

**Beat 4, Pattern**

Getting out of the car became a project I had to plan.

I could barely walk, get up and down out of a chair, the bed or the toilet.

It was horrible.

I'll be honest about the thing that humiliated me the most.

I could no longer put my own socks on without wanting to scream.

Sixty four years old and beaten by a sock.

But the nights were the worst part.

The absolute worst.

I couldn't lie on my right side anymore.

The second my hip touched the mattress it felt like someone pressing their thumb into a deep bruise.

So I'd roll to my left.

Then my left would start.

I'd wake up and not be able to go back to sleep.

I'd lay there and toss and turn, sometimes for hours.

Most nights I gave up around 4:45 and just sat in the dark kitchen with a heat pack, waiting for the sky to go grey.

**Beat 7, Emotional Low Point, at home**

I used to be the one who organised everything.

Family gatherings.

Birthdays.

Now I was the woman in the car park doing the maths on whether I could make it to the shops and back without having to stop and hold onto something.

My husband John started doing the grocery runs without telling me, so I wouldn't feel like I had to come along.

I noticed.

I didn't say anything.

But it broke something in me.

**Beat 6, Failed Attempts, the verdict**

So I did what you're supposed to do.

I went to the doctor.

He pressed on my hip, sent me for an X-ray, and a week later gave me the verdict.

Osteoarthritis.

The cartilage was wearing thin.

"It's fairly common at your age, Maggie."

At your age.

I wanted to scream.

He gave me anti inflammatories that wrecked my stomach and barely touched the pain.

When I went back, he sent me to an orthopaedic surgeon.

The surgeon looked at my scan for about ninety seconds.

Pointed at the gap on the screen.

Said the words I'll never forget.

"It's bone on bone. We'll want to replace that."

And just like that, I was on a waiting list.

Fourteen months.

He offered me a cortisone injection to get me through until then.

**Beat 7, Emotional Low Point, the date**

I drove home and cried in the driveway.

Not because of the surgery exactly.

Because of the date.

There was now a date hanging over my whole life, and a 14 month countdown of pain to survive before it.

**Beat 6, Failed Attempts, the 2am search**

That's when the 2am googling started.

If you've done it, you know exactly what I mean.

Phone screen too bright.

John asleep beside me.

Typing things like "hip pain can't sleep" and "bone on bone alternatives" and, on the dark nights, "how bad is hip replacement recovery."

I read everything.

I bought a TENS machine that did nothing.

I bought a special pillow.

I tried the physio exercises off the photocopied sheet, and I swear my hip got angrier, not better.

I was willing to try everything.

Stretches, tennis ball, over the counter meds, heat packs, physical therapy, anything to stop me wincing every time I stood up.

And still, nobody was answering the one question I actually had.

Why did this happen so fast, and is there anything that actually heals it instead of just numbing it.

**Beat 8, Bridge**

The answer came from the most unlikely place.

My sister in law, Denise.

A retired theatre nurse.

Sharp as a tack and not one for nonsense.

She came over for a cup of tea, watched me lever myself out of the armchair using both arms, and went quiet.

Then she said something that genuinely annoyed me at the time.

"Maggie, has anyone actually told you what's gone wrong in there, or did they just point at the bone?"

**Beat 10, Mechanism**

I said the surgeon told me.

Bone on bone.

And she said, "That's the joint. That's not why you can't sleep on your side. That's not why it stabs the second you stand up."

Then she explained the thing not one doctor had bothered to.

She said the sharp pain on the outside of the hip, the can't lie on it pain, very often isn't the cartilage at all.

It's the soft tissue around the joint.

The tendons that anchor the muscles to the outside of your hip.

She said as we get older, and especially for us women around menopause, those tendons lose their blood supply and their ability to repair themselves.

They get inflamed.

And then they get stuck in a loop where they simply stop healing.

Here's the part that made me sit up straight.

She said the reason the painkillers and the rest never worked was that none of them touched that.

They quieten the alarm.

They don't get fresh blood and repair into a starved tendon that has stopped fixing itself.

And you can't stretch your way out of it either, because loading an angry tendon just inflames it more.

Which explained, finally, why that physio sheet made everything worse.

For the first time in months, something actually made sense.

The problem wasn't only a worn out joint waiting for the scalpel.

The problem was tissue that had been starved of circulation and left stuck in inflammation, and nothing I'd tried was bringing it back to life.

**Beat 12, Differentiation**

So I went looking for the opposite of everything I'd been doing.

Not numbing.

Not masking.

Not loading it.

Something that would actually drive warmth and blood and repair into that exact spot.

**Beat 13, Product Introduction**

What I found was a therapy belt that wraps right around the hip and does four things at once.

Red and near infrared light that sinks down into the tissue.

Gentle heat.

A soft vibration massage.

And a light compression that holds it all exactly where the pain lives.

**Beat 18, Guarantee**

I'll be honest with you.

After everything that had failed, I expected nothing.

I only bought it because it came with a 30 day money back promise, so I figured I could send it back and feel smug about it.

**Beat 14, Personal Results**

The first night I used it for twenty minutes in my armchair while the news was on.

The warmth alone was the nicest my hip had felt in a year.

I went to bed thinking, well, at least that was pleasant.

I slept four hours on my right side.

The side I had not slept on in over a year.

I cried again.

A very different reason this time.

I won't pretend it was instant magic.

It wasn't.

But over the next few weeks, twenty minutes a day, the change was undeniable.

The morning stiffness got shorter.

The stab when I stepped off the kerb softened to a murmur, then most days, to nothing.

I got down on the floor with my granddaughter and got back up without the two armed push up off the couch.

I put my own socks on one morning and didn't even think about it until I was halfway out the door.

**Beat 14, the resolution**

By the time the hospital rang to confirm my surgery date, I sat there with the phone in my hand and realised I genuinely didn't want it anymore.

I think about what that 14 month wait nearly cost me.

Not just the operation.

The mornings with my grandkids I was too sore to enjoy.

The walks with John I kept saying no to.

The nights of sleep I will never get back.

That's the part that still gets me.

Not the pain.

The lost time.

**Beat 17, CTA**

I'm not a doctor.

I'm just a woman who was handed a date and a countdown, and stumbled onto the thing nobody had explained to me.

So if you're lying awake right now with that ache on the side of your hip, dreading a date on a calendar, I want you to know something.

The bone might not be the whole story.

There may be something starved and inflamed around that joint that nobody has properly looked at, and it may not be as finished as you have been told.

**Beat 19, Echo Close**

I wish someone had told me eighteen months ago.

So now I'm telling you.

Try it yourself.
