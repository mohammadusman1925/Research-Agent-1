10 · Mechanism

I said the surgeon told me. Bone on bone. And she said, “That's the joint. That's not why you can't sleep on your side. That's not why it stabs the second you stand up.” Then she explained the thing not one doctor had bothered to. She said the sharp pain on the outside of the hip, the can't lie on it pain, very often isn't the cartilage at all. It's the soft tissue around the joint. The tendons that anchor the muscles to the outside of your hip. She said as we get older, and especially for us women around menopause, those tendons lose their blood supply and their ability to repair themselves. They get inflamed. And then they get stuck in a loop where they simply stop healing. Here's the part that made me sit up straight. She said the reason the painkillers and the rest never worked was that none of them touched that. They quieten the alarm. They don't get fresh blood and repair into a starved tendon that has stopped fixing itself. And you can't stretch your way out of it either, because loading an angry tendon just inflames it more. Which explained, finally, why that physio sheet made everything worse. For the first time in months, something actually made sense. The problem wasn't only a worn out joint waiting for the scalpel. The problem was tissue that had been starved of circulation and left stuck in inflammation, and nothing I'd tried was bringing it back to life.

The image one per door

Every image in this account so far is text on a plain background. Zero symptom images have ever run, despite the copy handing over the sock, the letter, the heat pack. So each direction below is untested here. One image per script, no backups, no options. The method's own rule: show the most interesting concrete thing in the script. Never an idea, never a text overlay, and on cold traffic never the product, because the belt has not been earned yet.

The rule. The image comes from whatever the hook names. Not from the use case as a category, not from the pain point as a category. From the hook. That is why the method says finish the headline first: a changed headline voids its image direction.

If the hook namesThe image comes fromBeat
The situation she is inThe use case1 and 6
A thing she can no longer doThe pain point4
Why the last treatment failedThe explanation10
A number or a resultThe proof14
The offer or the priceThe product13

The gate that overrules it. Awareness. If the two disagree, awareness wins. Problem aware and cold: the use case or the pain point, and never the product because it has not been earned. Solution aware: the explanation, the diagram, the depth. Most aware: the product and the offer, never a symptom. Every script on this page is problem aware, so the belt never appears.

The pattern, four steps. 1. Read the hook. 2. Circle every noun you could photograph. 3. If there is more than one, take the most specific and the least expected. 4. That noun is the image, alone in the frame, nothing else.

HookThe noun it sticks toSource
“I was on a waiting list. Fourteen months.”the waiting listUse case

Two tests before you shoot. The swap test: could this image sit on any other ad in the category? If yes, it is not specific enough, go back to step 3. The loop test: does the image answer the question or ask it? It has to ask. An image that gives away the ending kills the reason to read.

KTA act on these

  1. Move one beat. If a second beat moved, you are not iterating, you are writing a new concept.
  2. Keep the bridge line into the body identical across every door. That is the seam, and it has to hold.
  3. Do not touch the echo close. This one is generic enough to survive all three openings, so leave it alone.
  4. Run all three doors at once against the control. Hooks are the cheapest thing you will ever test.
  5. Name them ITE and bump the version. Same angle code, same batch. Only the version moves.
  6. Judge on click through first, then CPA. A door can win attention and still lose the sale.
