The Three Biological Pathways

Adapted from the Metabolae structure (GLUT4/AMPK/NLRP3). Three named pathways that Haritaki's compounds act on, each backed by published research.

PATHWAY 1

XOD — Xanthine Oxidase

The enzyme that produces uric acid from purines. Haritaki inhibits XOD activity, reducing uric acid production at the source. Same target as allopurinol and febuxostat — prescription drugs for gout. (J Ethnopharmacol 2025; J Inflammation Research 2024)

PATHWAY 2

URAT1 / GLUT9 — Urate Transporters

Kidney transporters that reabsorb uric acid back into the blood instead of flushing it out. Haritaki downregulates both, promoting excretion. Less reabsorption = lower serum uric acid. (Li et al., 2025, J Ethnopharmacol)

PATHWAY 3

NLRP3 / NF-kB — Inflammatory Cascade

Uric acid crystals trigger the NLRP3 inflammasome in kidney tubular cells, releasing IL-1beta and IL-18. Haritaki suppresses both NLRP3 activation and the NF-kB pathway that amplifies it. The alarm gets turned off. (J Ethnopharmacol 2024; PMC 38159820)

Appendix B: Compliance Notes

Risk areaSpecific riskMitigation
FDA / FTC (health claims) Kidney disease claims. CKD treatment or prevention claims. GFR/creatinine improvement claims. Structure/function claims only. "Supports healthy kidney function." Never: "treats," "prevents," "cures" kidney disease. All claims hedged: "may help," "studies suggest," "research shows." Include FDA disclaimer on all content.
Clinical evidence gap Kidney-specific Haritaki research is animal models. Human RCT measured uric acid, not kidney function. The RCT tested a different extract than Lanaya's. Always disclose: "The kidney research includes experimental animal studies." "The human trial measured serum uric acid using a specific extract and was not a clinical trial of Lanaya." Never imply Lanaya was the product tested.
Meta ad policy Health claims. Before/after. Personal attributes ("you have kidney disease"). Body image. Fear-based medical claims. No before/after framing. No "you have [condition]." Use "people with..." or "if your doctor mentioned..." instead. No fear-based medical imagery. Education-first framing reduces policy risk.
Testimonials FTC requires "typical results" disclosure. Fabricated reviews are illegal under FTC Endorsement Guides (updated 2023). Only use verified customer language. If no verified reviews exist (current Lanaya situation), do not fabricate. Build real testimonial pipeline from actual purchasers. Flag: Lanaya's existing on-site reviews appear templated.
Competitor comparison claims NAD/BBB challenges on comparative advertising. Lanham Act. Compare categories, not named brands. "Most kidney supplements" vs naming VitaPost or Renew Life. Factual ingredient/dose comparisons only.
Doctor / nurse narrators FTC requires actual credentials if claiming authority. Fake doctors are prosecuted. If using a nurse or doctor narrator, they endorse the result, never recommend the product. Credential must be real or clearly fictional ("a nurse I follow" vs "Dr. Smith recommends"). No white coat imagery without real credentials.
