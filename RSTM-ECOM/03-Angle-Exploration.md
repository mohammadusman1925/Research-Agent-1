## Angle Cluster 1: The Operator Advantage

### Angle 1A: "We Use What We Sell"

**Thesis:** RSTM operates their own brands. Unlike agencies, they eat their own cooking. Creative that shows the internal team using, testing, and iterating on their own products builds trust that no agency testimonial can match.

**Why It Works:** RSTM's hybrid model is rare. Most competitors are either pure agencies (no skin in the game) or pure operators (no outside expertise). RSTM has both — and that story is untold in their current creative.

**Formats:**
- **Video/UGC:** Behind-the-scenes of the team testing products, packing orders, reviewing data
- **Static:** Split-screen "Agency says vs. We actually did" comparisons
- **Native:** Blog/article content showing real operational decisions and their outcomes

**Metrics:** Hook rate (does the "insider access" hook grab attention?), Hold rate (does the story keep them?), CTR (does credibility drive clicks?)

**Risk:** If the behind-the-scenes production quality is too polished, it loses the authenticity that makes it work. Must feel real, not staged.

---

### Angle 1B: "Built on €15M in Real Results"

**Thesis:** RSTM isn't theorizing. €15M+ in revenue, 100% YoY growth, 150+ high-converting pages. Lead with proof, not promises.

**Why It Works:** The DTC space is drowning in agencies claiming results. RSTM has verifiable numbers from their own operations — not client case studies that can be inflated.

**Formats:**
- **Video:** Data-reveal format — start with a hook about "what most agencies won't show you," then reveal actual metrics
- **Static:** Bold number-forward graphics (€15M+, 100% growth, 150+ pages)
- **UGC:** Michael or team members casually referencing real numbers in conversation-style content

**Metrics:** CTR (do proof-based hooks drive more clicks than benefit-based?), CPA (does credibility lower acquisition cost?), CPM (does Meta's algorithm favor authority content?)

**Risk:** Numbers without context feel like bragging. Must tie each metric to a customer outcome, not just company achievement.

## Angle Cluster 2: The Sustainability Story (Mission-Driven)

### Angle 2A: "Sustainably Sourced Isn't a Label — It's How We Build"

**Thesis:** RSTM's mission centers on "sustainably sourced products." But in 2026, every brand claims sustainability. The angle is showing the PROCESS, not the claim.

**Why It Works:** European consumers (RSTM's primary market) are more sophisticated about sustainability claims. They've seen greenwashing. What they haven't seen is transparent supply chain storytelling from an eCom operator.

**Formats:**
- **Video:** Supply chain documentary-style — sourcing, production, packaging decisions and why
- **Static:** Ingredient/material origin maps with real supplier details
- **UGC:** Unboxing content that highlights sustainable packaging choices and explains the trade-offs

**Metrics:** Hold rate (sustainability content either deeply engages or immediately loses people — there's no middle), Engagement rate, Share rate (sustainability content that resonates gets shared)

**Risk:** Sustainability content that's too educational becomes boring. Must lead with emotion or surprise, then deliver the proof.

---

### Angle 2B: "The Products We'd Buy Even If We Didn't Own the Brand"

**Thesis:** The team personally uses these products. That's the ultimate proof of product quality — and it's a story no pure agency can tell.

**Why It Works:** Bridges the gap between "company promoting its products" and "real people who genuinely use this." The operator model makes this authentic in a way competitors can't replicate.

**Formats:**
- **UGC:** Team members showing their actual daily use — morning routines, office use, personal favorites
- **Video:** "Why I started using [product] before I worked here" narratives
- **Static:** "Team favorites" series — personal picks with genuine reasons

**Metrics:** Hook rate, Trust metrics (comments, saves, DMs), Conversion rate (does personal endorsement from the team convert differently than standard ads?)

**Risk:** Can feel forced if the team isn't genuinely enthusiastic. Only works with products the team actually uses.

## Angle Cluster 3: The Testing Culture

### Angle 3A: "Always Testing, Always Learning" — The Philosophy as Content

**Thesis:** Michael's core philosophy isn't just internal culture — it's a content angle. Showing the testing process (what was tried, what failed, what worked) positions RSTM as transparent and scientific in a space full of guesswork.

**Why It Works:** The DTC audience (both consumers and potential partners) is tired of brands that only show wins. Showing the testing process — including failures — builds credibility and engagement.

**Formats:**
- **Video:** "We tested 47 versions of this ad — here's what won and why" (meta-content about creative testing)
- **Static:** Before/after creative comparisons with real performance data
- **Native:** Weekly or monthly "what we learned" content series

**Metrics:** Engagement rate, Save rate (testing content gets saved for reference), Follow rate (people follow accounts that teach, not just sell)

**Risk:** Revealing too much of the testing process could help competitors. Must balance transparency with competitive advantage.

---

### Angle 3B: "The Science Behind Your Feed"

**Thesis:** Most brands create ads. RSTM engineers them. Content about WHY certain sounds, visuals, hooks, and formats work — backed by data — positions them as the smartest operator in the room.

**Why It Works:** The eCom market is saturated with "tips and tricks" content. What's missing is content that goes deeper — explaining the algorithm mechanics, the psychology behind hook rates, why CPM moves when you change a sound.

**Formats:**
- **Video:** Educational explainers — "Why your CPM is high (and it's not your audience)" / "The 3-second rule that doubles hold rate"
- **Static:** Data visualization — before/after metrics from creative changes
- **Native:** LinkedIn-style thought leadership from Michael or the Creative Strategist

**Metrics:** CPM (educational content often gets lower CPMs because Meta rewards engagement), Share rate, Comment rate

**Risk:** Can become too "guru-like" if not anchored to specific, verifiable results. Every claim must have a data point behind it.

## Angle Cluster 4: European Market Sophistication

### Angle 4A: "What Works in the US Won't Work Here"

**Thesis:** European eCom consumers have different expectations, cultural context, and buying behavior. RSTM understands this because they operate IN European markets, not from the outside.

**Why It Works:** Most creative strategy frameworks are US-centric. RSTM's Austrian base and European market operation gives them a genuine edge in understanding local nuance.

**Formats:**
- **Video:** Side-by-side ad comparisons: US version vs. European-adapted version, with performance data
- **Static:** Market-by-market creative insights — what each European market responds to differently
- **Native:** Case studies of creative adaptation across markets

**Metrics:** CPM by market (does localized creative reduce CPM?), CTR by market, Conversion rate by market

**Risk:** Could alienate US audience if positioned as "US is wrong." Better framed as "different markets need different creative intelligence."

---

### Angle 4B: "One Product, Five Markets, Five Different Stories"

**Thesis:** The same product needs fundamentally different creative approaches across Germany, Austria, Nordic countries, and other European markets. Show this process.

**Why It Works:** This is a capability gap most competitors can't fill. A Creative Strategist who can map the same product to different cultural entry points is exponentially more valuable than one who writes one brief and translates it.

**Formats:**
- **Video:** "How we sell the same product in 5 different ways" — real creative examples from different markets
- **Static:** Cultural insight cards — one product, multiple market-specific hooks
- **UGC:** Market-specific UGC creators showing authentic local context

**Metrics:** Per-market performance comparison, Overall ROAS across markets, Creative efficiency (revenue per creative produced)

**Risk:** Requires actual multi-market data to be credible. Cannot be theoretical — must show real results from real markets.

## Angle Cluster 5: The Scale Play

### Angle 5A: "From €15M to €100M — The Creative System That Gets Us There"

**Thesis:** RSTM has a public €100M target. The creative system required to reach that number is fundamentally different from what got them to €15M. Content about building that system IS the positioning.

**Why It Works:** Scale stories create authority. Sharing the roadmap (not just the destination) makes RSTM the protagonist in a growth narrative that audiences want to follow.

**Formats:**
- **Video:** Quarterly progress updates — real numbers, real challenges, real decisions
- **Native:** Long-form content about the operational changes required at each revenue tier
- **Static:** Milestone graphics — where they are on the path to €100M

**Metrics:** Brand awareness (measured through search volume and direct traffic), Partnership inquiries, Recruitment pipeline quality

**Risk:** If growth stalls, the narrative becomes a liability. Must be honest about setbacks, not just wins.
