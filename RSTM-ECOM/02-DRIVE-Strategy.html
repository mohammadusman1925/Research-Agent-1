DRIVE Creative Strategy — Applied to RSTM Portfolio

A complete creative strategy framework applied to RSTM's three DTC brands. Each phase builds on real market data, verified VOC, and evidence-backed creative principles. Primary demonstration: MamaWillow. Secondary: HappyHund & ShareSwiss.

DRIVE: Discover the mass desire → Reframe the problem → Ignite emotional response → Variate through testing → Evolve via documented learning. This framework turns consumer insight into a repeatable creative engine — not one good ad, but a system that produces them.

D — DISCOVER

Phase 1: Discover the Mass Desire

Discover maps what people already want before we say a single word. We don't create desire — we channel existing desire toward the product. This phase identifies the mass desire, maps market sophistication, and profiles the humans who feel it most intensely.

1.1 Mass Desire Analysis

Every brand in RSTM's portfolio taps into a deeper desire than the product itself. People don't buy laundry sheets, dog supplements, or fermented plums. They buy what those products represent.

BrandProductSurface wantMass desire (the real purchase)
MamaWillowEco laundry sheets"Clean clothes without chemicals"To be a good parent who protects their family AND the planet without sacrificing convenience. The identity desire: "I'm the kind of person who makes better choices."
HappyHundDog health chews"Help my dog's joints"To not fail the creature that depends on me completely. The guilt desire: "I'd do anything for my dog — I just need to know what 'anything' actually is."
ShareSwissFermented wellness plums"Improve gut health"To finally find the thing that actually works after everything else failed. The hope desire: "Maybe this one is different."
"The power in your advertising will always come from channeling an existing desire, not from creating a new one."— Eugene Schwartz, Breakthrough Advertising

Creative Implication

  • MamaWillow (Stage 4): Cannot lead with "eco-friendly" alone. Must lead with proof (certifications, dissolution demo, ingredient transparency) or identity ("Join 5,000+ households that switched"). Greenwashing skepticism is the wall to breach.
  • HappyHund (Stage 4): Cannot lead with "glucosamine for joints." Every competitor says that. Must lead with a unique mechanism (bioavailability, specific formulation) or authority proof (named veterinarian, clinical reference).
  • ShareSwiss (Stage 3): Can still lead with mechanism — "Japanese fermentation" is novel enough in DACH markets to differentiate. But must escalate quickly. The window for mechanism-first copy closes as competitors copy the language.

R — REFRAME

Phase 2: Reframe the Problem

Reframe shifts how the prospect sees their problem. We don't compete on the same battlefield as everyone else — we change the battlefield. The prospect walks in thinking one thing is wrong. They leave understanding a different thing is wrong — one that only our product solves.

2.1 Problem Mechanism — MamaWillow (Primary)

The conventional framing of eco-cleaning: "Chemicals are bad for the planet."

The problem with this framing: everyone says it. It's Stage 4 saturated. 52% of consumers assume it's greenwashing.

The Reframe: The problem isn't chemicals in your detergent. The problem is the bottle itself.

Conventional detergent is 80-90% water. You're paying to ship water across Europe in a plastic bottle that takes 450 years to decompose. Eco brands that put "natural" detergent in the same plastic bottles haven't solved anything — they've just made you feel better about buying the same problem.

MamaWillow eliminated the bottle entirely. Not because it's trendy. Because the bottle was the problem all along.

2.2 Problem Mechanism — HappyHund

The Reframe: The problem isn't that your dog needs supplements. The problem is that modern dog food strips out the nutrients dogs evolved to get from raw food.

Wild canids eat raw bone, cartilage, and organ meat — natural sources of glucosamine, MSM, and collagen. Kibble processes these nutrients out. Your dog isn't deficient because of age. Your dog is deficient because of diet. HappyHund puts back what modern food took away.

2.3 Problem Mechanism — ShareSwiss

The Reframe: The problem isn't that probiotics don't work. The problem is that supplements bypass the digestion process that makes nutrients absorbable.

Capsules deliver bacteria to your gut. Fermented foods deliver bacteria that have already broken down nutrients into bioavailable form — because that's what fermentation IS. The Japanese have known this for a thousand years. We just put it in a pill-sized plum.

2.6 Four-Line Reframe Chains

A Reframe Chain walks the prospect from their current belief to a new one in exactly four lines. Line 1: Acknowledge their current belief. Line 2: Introduce the crack. Line 3: Reframe. Line 4: Bridge to the product.

MamaWillow — 3 Variations

Reframe Chain 1: "The Bottle Problem"

Line 1: You switched to eco-friendly detergent. Good choice. (Validate)
Line 2: But your "eco" detergent is still 80% water in a plastic bottle that takes 450 years to break down. (Crack)
Line 3: The problem was never the detergent. It was the bottle. (Reframe)
Line 4: MamaWillow eliminated the bottle entirely. One sheet. Same clean. Nothing to throw away. (Bridge)

Reframe Chain 2: "The Weight Revelation"

Line 1: You're paying €8 for 2kg of laundry detergent. (Validate)
Line 2: 1.8kg of that is water. You're paying €7.20 to ship water to your house. (Crack)
Line 3: What if you could get just the cleaning power — without paying for someone else's water? (Reframe)
Line 4: MamaWillow packs a full load of cleaning into a sheet thinner than a credit card. €0.66 per wash. (Bridge)

Reframe Chain 3: "The Skin Question"

Line 1: You read every ingredient on your food labels. (Validate)
Line 2: But do you know what's in the detergent that touches your children's skin 24 hours a day? (Crack)
Line 3: Your skin absorbs what it touches. Your children's skin absorbs it faster. (Reframe)
Line 4: MamaWillow: every ingredient listed, nothing hidden, nothing synthetic. Founded by a mom who asked the same question. (Bridge)

HappyHund — 3 Variations

Reframe Chain 1: "The Missing Nutrients"

Line 1: You feed your dog premium kibble. You think they're getting everything they need. (Validate)
Line 2: But processing removes the glucosamine, collagen, and MSM that dogs once got from raw bone and cartilage. (Crack)
Line 3: Your dog isn't aging badly. Your dog's food is missing what nature used to provide. (Reframe)
Line 4: HappyHund puts back what kibble takes out. Grain-free chews your dog thinks are treats. (Bridge)

Line 1: Your dog seems fine. Plays, eats, wags their tail. (Validate)
Line 2: But joint cartilage doesn't have nerve endings. By the time your dog shows pain, 60% of the joint surface is already damaged. (Crack)
Line 3: You're not waiting for the right time to start. You're waiting past it. (Reframe)
Line 4: HappyHund. Vet-recommended prevention your dog will beg for. (Bridge)

Reframe Chain 3: "The Cost Reframe"

Line 1: Dog joint supplements cost €25-45 a month. That feels like a lot. (Validate)
Line 2: A single vet visit for joint problems costs €200-800. Surgery: €2,000-5,000. (Crack)
Line 3: The question isn't "Can I afford supplements?" It's "Can I afford not to?" (Reframe)
Line 4: HappyHund. Less than €1.50 a day. Prevention your dog loves. (Bridge)

V — VARIATE

Phase 4: Variate Through Systematic Testing

Variate is not "make more ads." It's a structured testing architecture that isolates variables, documents results, and compounds learning across all three brands. One concept becomes 30 variations. 50 variations find 6-9 winners. Those winners inform the next 50.

4.1 Testing Architecture — The 6-Level Ladder

Each level tests one variable at a time. Move to the next level only when the current level has a winner. Never test two variables simultaneously — you won't know which one caused the result.

4FormatSame concept, different format (video, carousel, static, text-wall, comparison)4 per winning conceptCTR
5AngleSame product, different entry point (pain, aspiration, comparison, education, story)5 per productCPA

E — EVOLVE

Phase 5: Evolve Through Documented Learning

Evolve is what separates a creative strategist from a content creator. It's the system that makes every test smarter than the last. Not "we tried this and it worked." Rather: "we know WHY it worked, where else it applies, and what to test next because of it."
