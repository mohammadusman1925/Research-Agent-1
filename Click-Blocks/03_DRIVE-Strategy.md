## PART 1: DISCOVER

### 1.1 Mass Desire Analysis

The mass desire is NOT "magnetic building blocks."

**The mass desire is:** "I want my kid to put the screen down and be a kid again — without me having to fight for it."

This desire predates Click Blocks. It's the same desire that drives parents to buy educational toys, enforce screen-time rules, sign kids up for activities, and lie awake at 2am wondering if their child's brain is being rewired by YouTube Shorts.

| Dimension | Score | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Urgency | HIGH | The guilt is daily. Every handed-over iPad is a fresh reminder. |
| Staying power | HIGH | Screen time anxiety grows as children age and devices multiply. |
| Scope | HIGH | Every parent on social media is addressable. Universal anxiety. |

**Amplifying forces:**
- Schools sending home screen-time warning newsletters
- Paediatricians flagging attention span concerns
- Social media showing "perfect" screen-free households (comparison guilt)
- Rising ADHD diagnosis rates linked to screen use in parenting discourse
- Parents' own phone addiction projected onto fears for their kids

### 1.4 ICP Profiles

#### ICP 1: The Guilty Scroller (Primary)
- **Trigger event:** Caught herself handing over the iPad for the third time today.
- **Mental state:** "I know it's bad. I keep saying 'just 20 more minutes' and it turns into two hours."
- **What she's tried:** Screen-time limits (meltdowns). Taking devices away (tantrums). Buying toys (cupboard after day one).
- **Awareness:** Problem-Aware. Doesn't know a toy can fight back using dopamine.
- **Sophistication:** Stage 3.
- **Dominant trigger:** Freedom from guilt.
- **Belief blocking the sale:** "No toy can compete with a screen."
- **Emotional doorway:** Guilt → Relief.

#### ICP 2: The Developmental Worrier (Primary)
- **Trigger event:** Noticed her child can't sit still without a device. Teacher mentioned attention issues.
- **Mental state:** "His attention span is shot. What have I done?"
- **What she's tried:** More outdoor time. Reading time. Educational apps (still a screen).
- **Awareness:** Problem-Aware, edging Solution-Aware.
- **Sophistication:** Stage 2–3.
- **Dominant trigger:** Protection of child's development.
- **Belief blocking the sale:** "The damage might already be done."
- **Emotional doorway:** Fear → Hope.

#### ICP 3: The Toy Graveyard Parent (Secondary)
- **Trigger event:** Just cleaned out the playroom. Found $300 worth of toys played with once.
- **Mental state:** "I'm done wasting money on toys they don't use."
- **What she's tried:** Expensive educational toys, LEGO sets, arts & crafts.
- **Awareness:** Solution-Aware. Doesn't believe any toy will work.
- **Sophistication:** Stage 3–4.
- **Dominant trigger:** Superiority ("I'm a smart buyer").
- **Belief blocking the sale:** "Every toy company says their product is different. None of them are."
- **Emotional doorway:** Cynicism → Surprise.

#### ICP 4: The ADHD/Neurodiverse Parent (Segment)
- **Trigger event:** Searching for sensory toys or focus aids for a neurodivergent child.
- **Mental state:** "Nothing holds his attention. Only the screen works and I hate it."
- **Awareness:** Solution-Aware. Actively searching.
- **Sophistication:** Stage 3.
- **Dominant trigger:** Care and protection.
- **Belief blocking the sale:** "This isn't designed for kids like mine."
- **Emotional doorway:** Exhaustion → Discovery.

#### ICP 5: The Gift-Giver (Seasonal)
- **Trigger event:** Birthday or Christmas approaching. Wants something meaningful.
- **Mental state:** "I want to give them something they'll actually use."
- **Awareness:** Unaware to Problem-Aware.
- **Sophistication:** Stage 2.
- **Dominant trigger:** Social approval + care.
- **Belief blocking the sale:** "Will they actually play with this?"
- **Emotional doorway:** Uncertainty → Confidence.

## PART 2: REFRAME

### 2.1 Problem Mechanism

- **Surface problem:** "My kid won't put the screen down."
- **True cause:** Screens deliver constant micro-dopamine rewards. Every toy competes against this and loses because toys don't have a reward loop built in.
- **Why this matters:** Any toy without its own dopamine reward loop will fail — no matter how educational or expensive.
- **The knowledge gap:** Most parents blame themselves or the child. Once they understand the neurochemical mechanism, the problem shifts from personal failure to a chemistry problem with a chemistry solution.

### 2.2 Why Other Solutions Fail

| Alternative | What parents believe | Why it fails |
|---|---|---|
| Taking screens away | "They'll find something else to do." | Removing dopamine source without alternative triggers withdrawal (tantrums). |
| Screen time limits | "30 minutes a day is reasonable." | Creates a scarcity loop — child spends non-screen time anticipating screen time. |
| Educational toys | "STEM on the box = better than iPad." | Front-load learning, back-load reward. Screens do the opposite. |
| LEGO / construction kits | "Building is creative and screen-free." | Has a finish line. Once built, dopamine stops. Set goes on shelf. |
| Arts & crafts | "Drawing and painting are wholesome." | Require supervision, create mess, and reward is delayed. |
| Willpower / guilt | "I just need to be a better parent." | You're fighting a dopamine system designed by the best engineers in the world. |

### 2.3 Category Gap Analysis

| What everyone says (commodity) | What no one says (gap) |
|---|---|
| "Educational," "STEM," "screen-free" | "Why your child's brain chooses the screen over every toy" |
| "Hours of creative play" | "The sound that hijacks the same reward loop the screen uses" |
| "Loved by parents and kids" | "He sat there for two hours. Without asking for the iPad once." |
| "Magnetic building blocks" | "A toy that can't be completed — because the dopamine never stops" |

### 2.4 Solution Mechanism

**Layer 1: The Dopamine Click** — Magnetic snap sound delivers a micro-dopamine reward every time two pieces connect. Build → click → dopamine → build more.

**Layer 2: Open-Ended Architecture** — No instructions. No finish line. Infinite configurations. The dopamine loop never terminates.

**Layer 3: Narrative Layer** — Included characters transform construction into storytelling. Engages language, imagination, and social cognition simultaneously.

### 2.5 Reframe Chains

**Variation 1: Logical / Educational**
1. Most parents think their child prefers screens because screens are more interesting than toys.
2. But the real reason is neurochemical: screens deliver micro-dopamine rewards every few seconds.
3. That's why every toy you've bought ends up in the cupboard.
4. Click Blocks work because every magnetic snap delivers the same dopamine micro-reward.

**Variation 2: Emotional / Story-Based**
1. I used to hand my kid the iPad just to get 20 minutes of peace.
2. I'd buy toys. Nice ones. They'd sit in a cupboard after day one.
3. Then I found out WHY. Screens deliver dopamine faster. No toy could match that speed.
4. Until these. The click sound gives them a tiny hit of dopamine — keeping them building for hours.

**Variation 3: Provocative / Pattern-Interrupt**
1. You're not a bad parent. You're fighting a dopamine system designed by Silicon Valley.
2. Every app is engineered to deliver rewards faster than real life can.
3. Taking the screen away doesn't fix it. Buying "educational" toys doesn't fix it.
4. One toy does. Every magnetic snap = a micro-reward. Same chemistry, different direction.

## PART 5: EVOLVE

### 5.1 Testing Hierarchy

| Test level | What changes | Kill threshold |
|---|---|---|
| Hook test | First 3 seconds only | Below 25% hook retention at 3s |
| Angle test | The pain point / entry emotion | CPA 2× above target after $200 spend |
| Format test | UGC vs B-roll vs ASMR vs timelapse | CTR below 1.5% after 10K impressions |
| Body test | Mechanism depth, proof sequence | Completion rate below 15% at 30s |
| CTA/offer test | Price framing, guarantee language | CVR below 1.5% on landing page |

### 5.2 Fatigue Signals

- **Frequency > 3.0:** Creative fatigue. Rotate to a new hook.
- **CTR declining 15%+ week-over-week:** The hook is dying. New creative needed.
- **CPM rising with stable CTR:** Audience saturation. Expand targeting or test new ICP.
- **Comment sentiment shifting negative:** Over-exposure. Pull and relaunch with fresh variation.

### 5.3 Scale Triggers

- **CPA at or below target for 3 consecutive days:** Increase budget 20% daily.
- **Hook retention above 40% at 3s:** Create 3 body variations under it.
- **ROAS above 2.5× at $500+ spend:** Scale hard. This is a winner.
- **Comment section generating organic UGC:** Boost and reshare.

### 5.4 Sophistication Escalation Plan
