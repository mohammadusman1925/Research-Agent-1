## THE MECHANISM (What Makes It Different)

Click Blocks positions around ONE unique mechanism:

**"The Dopamine Click"** -- Every time blocks snap together they produce a satisfying magnetic clicking sound. This gives children a small dopamine reward, which keeps them playing. The brand explicitly states this is "the same psychology used by big tech companies to hold their attention to the devices."

This is their NEW/ONLY positioning. They are not selling "magnetic tiles" (categorization = death). They are selling the first toy that uses the screen's own dopamine trick against it.

**Supporting differentiators:**
- Open-ended play (cannot be "completed" -- no finish line = no dust-collecting)
- Forces imagination (no rules, no instructions -- kids decide what to build)
- Characters included (sparks storytelling, not just construction)
- Recommended by teachers and schools (authority/SAFE signal)

## TARGET AVATAR

**Primary:** Mums aged 28-45 with kids aged 3-12.
**Secondary:** Grandparents buying gifts, dads, teachers.

**Psychographic profile of the primary avatar:**
- Feels guilty about how much screen time their kids get
- Has tried taking screens away and it ended in meltdowns
- Buys toys that get played with for 10 minutes then forgotten
- Wants their kid to be creative, imaginative, and learning -- not zombified
- Scrolls social media themselves (irony they're aware of)
- Values "the way things used to be" -- outdoor play, imagination, building forts
- Concerned about attention span, social skills, and developmental milestones

## COMPETITIVE LANDSCAPE

**Direct competitors:** Magna-Tiles, PicassoTiles, Playmags, LEGO (loosely)

**Click Blocks' positioning advantage:**
- Cheaper than Magna-Tiles ($24-$52 vs $50-$120+)
- Includes characters (storytelling > pure construction)
- "Dopamine click" mechanism is named and owned
- Schools endorsement angle (authority)
- Subscription model (recurring revenue, ongoing engagement)

**Risk signals found in research:**
- Some scam-checker sites flagged low trust scores (common for newer DTC Shopify brands)
- Some Trustpilot reviews mention delivery delays and smaller-than-expected block sizes
- These are worth noting but do NOT change the ad strategy -- address through guarantee/risk-reversal in CTA

## AWARENESS & SOPHISTICATION ASSESSMENT

**Market Awareness: Problem-Aware (Stage 4 per Schwartz)**
Parents KNOW screen time is a problem. They feel it daily. They do NOT know that a toy exists that uses the screen's own dopamine psychology against it. They need to be walked from Problem-Aware to Solution-Aware to Product-Aware within the ad.

**Market Sophistication: Stage 2-3**
The "magnetic toy" category exists but is not saturated with direct-response advertising. Most competitors sell through retail/Amazon with feature-based messaging. Click Blocks can still make a direct claim ("the first toy that uses screen dopamine against itself") and be believed. We are at the stage where introducing the MECHANISM is the winning move.

**Script Flow (per Evolve system):** Problem > Solution > Product > Offer

## THE FIVE PAIN POINTS CONSIDERED

1. **Screen time guilt & daily battles** -- The daily war over tablets and phones. The guilt of knowing it's bad but having no alternative that works.
2. **Toys that collect dust** -- Buying expensive toys that get 10 minutes of play then sit in a cupboard forever.
3. **Loss of imagination/creativity** -- Kids can't entertain themselves without a device. The digital world does the thinking for them.
4. **ADHD/neurodivergent engagement** -- Finding something that holds attention for special needs kids.
5. **Sibling conflict over devices** -- Fighting over who gets the iPad, no collaborative play.
