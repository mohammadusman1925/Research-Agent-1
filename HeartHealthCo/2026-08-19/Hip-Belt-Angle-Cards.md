# Hip Belt -- Angle Cards for Testing

**Product:** Hip Therapy Belt | Code: HHCH
**Control:** "Beaten By A Sock" (Maggie, native article, ~1,100 words)
**Guardrails:** Do NOT repeat bone on bone / arthritis / hip replacement. Identity validation, never antagonism. Soft close. 30-day guarantee inside the story.
**Buyer:** 65+ is 51.6% of purchases. Buyer is older than instinct suggests.
**Open clusters:** PC02 Medication Liberation (no winner), PC03 Mobility and Activity Loss (no winner), Piriformis/Sciatica (Pete asked, nothing drafted)

## ANGLE 01 -- The Cortisone Clock

**Concept:** You only get so many shots before they stop offering them. Each injection is a countdown, not a solution. The body is being managed, not healed. *One-word: Countdown*

**Angle:** The belief shift is that cortisone is not treatment -- it is a delay tactic with an expiration date. The tissue around the joint stays starved while the clock runs. *Emotion: the runway is getting shorter and nobody told you.*

**Use Case:** The injection appointment -- she is in the car park after her second cortisone shot. The first one lasted four months. This one lasted six weeks. The receptionist has already booked the third. She is doing the maths in her head.

**Hypothesis:** PC02 Medication Liberation has 9 angle cards and no winner. Cortisone is the villain with the most heat in the review corpus and customers reach that conclusion themselves. This tests whether the countdown framing -- a finite number of shots running out -- converts better than a general "cortisone doesn't work" message.

**Sources:** "injections twice which were reasonably effective for around 3 months (then wore off)" -- Gransnet. "Cortisone / steroid injections" at 22 mentions in VOC. "each year, it got a little harder" -- Recovery Blog.

## ANGLE 02 -- The Slip-On Life

**Concept:** When you switch from lace-ups to slip-ons, you are not choosing comfort. You are surrendering a capability. Every small adaptation is a concession that becomes permanent. *One-word: Surrender*

**Angle:** The belief shift is that the body adapts around the pain by shrinking what you ask of it -- and those workarounds become the new ceiling. The tissue needs demand, not avoidance. *Emotion: the slow fade of capability that nobody else notices.*

**Use Case:** The shoe rack -- she is standing at the door, looking at the runners she used to wear. The slip-ons are already on her feet. She did not even consider the lace-ups. Her granddaughter is waiting at the car.

**Hypothesis:** "Switched to slip-on shoes" appeared 14 times in VOC -- a behaviour change people describe with resignation, not anger. This tests whether a small, specific object (the shoe) can carry the weight of a larger mobility-loss story in a way that the broad "can't walk" framing cannot.

**Sources:** "I couldn't tie my shoes and swapped to slip ons" -- BoneSmart. "couldn't get flipping sock on right foot" -- BoneSmart. "Putting on shoes / socks" at 22 mentions.

## ANGLE 05 -- The Piriformis Nobody Mentioned

**Concept:** You went to three appointments and nobody said the word piriformis. The thing sitting on your sciatic nerve has a name, a location, and a reason it tightened. You were treated for the symptom. The cause was never addressed. *One-word: Overlooked*

**Angle:** The belief shift is that sciatica is the symptom, not the diagnosis. The piriformis muscle compresses the nerve when the surrounding tissue is tight and underserved. Treating the nerve without addressing the muscle is managing the alarm, not the fire. *Emotion: betrayal by omission -- the information existed and nobody offered it.*

**Use Case:** The GP waiting room, third visit -- she is holding a new referral slip. The last two said "sciatica, try physio." She is googling piriformis on her phone in the waiting room. She found the word on a forum, not from a doctor.

**Hypothesis:** Pete asked about piriformis and sciatica, nothing drafted. "Piriformis syndrome" at 12 mentions, "Sciatica" at 42 mentions (highest single term in hip VOC). This tests whether naming the overlooked muscle -- giving the buyer a word they did not have -- creates a stronger mechanism entry than the general sciatica framing.

**Sources:** "piriformis muscle in the buttocks presses on the sciatic nerve" -- Medical forums. "had sciatica for several years before having it properly diagnosed" -- HealthUnlocked. "Sciatica" at 42 mentions.

## ANGLE 08 -- The Staircase Negotiation

**Angle:** The belief shift is that stair pain is a soft-tissue failure, not a bone failure. The muscles that stabilise the hip on incline have lost their elastic capacity. ## ANGLE 21 -- The Bone That's Not The Problem

**Concept:** The X-ray showed the bone. The doctor pointed at the bone. The diagnosis was about the bone. But the pain is in the tissue around it -- the capsule, the muscles, the fascia -- and those do not show up on an X-ray. She was diagnosed on half the evidence. *One-word: Invisible*

**Angle:** The belief shift is that imaging shows structure, not function. The tissue that generates pain signals -- when it is tight, starved, or inflamed -- is invisible on standard imaging. The diagnosis is incomplete, and that is why the treatment did not work. *Emotion: the revelation that the diagnosis itself was the wrong answer.*

**Use Case:** The radiology waiting room -- she is looking at the disc with her scans. The report says "mild degeneration, age-appropriate." She does not feel age-appropriate. She feels like something is wrong that they are not seeing.

**Hypothesis:** This is the mechanism closest to the control ("the bone is not the whole story; the tissue is starved and stuck"). This tests whether leading with the diagnostic gap -- what the X-ray missed -- is a stronger doorway than leading with a scene, because it reframes the entire medical experience rather than one moment of it.

**Sources:** "endure months of pain and physical therapy before a correct diagnosis" -- Recovery Blog. "It was bone on bone. Once I saw the images, I knew it wasn't going to fix itself" -- Recovery Blog. "Complex hip pain with no answers" -- Mayo Clinic Connect.
