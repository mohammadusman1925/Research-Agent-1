# Shoulder Belt -- Angle Cards for Testing

**Product:** Shoulder Therapy Belt
**Control:** None established. All angles are NN (new concepts).
**Buyer signals from VOC:** Age 40-60 dominant, recreational lifters, desk workers, parents, female skew. Frozen shoulder and rotator cuff are the two highest-frequency conditions.

---

## ANGLE 01 -- The Shirt Test

**Concept:** She does not put on a shirt anymore. She steps into it. The arms go through from the bottom. The neck goes over last. It is a system she invented at 3am after the fifth night of trying. She has not worn a pullover since March. *One-word: System*

**Angle:** The belief shift is that dressing pain is not about the arm -- it is about the shoulder capsule tissue being too tight and starved to allow the overhead rotation that putting on a shirt requires. The movement is natural; the tissue is not. *Emotion: the private humiliation of struggling with something a child can do.*

**Use Case:** The bedroom, 6:45am -- she is holding a button-down shirt. The t-shirt she wanted to wear is still in the drawer. She chose the button-down because it opens in the front. She does not think about this choice anymore. She just makes it.

**Hypothesis:** "Putting on a shirt / getting dressed" at 12 mentions, tied for 3rd highest in shoulder VOC. "intense enough that I get tears in my eyes when I put on a shirt" is the most visceral verbatim in the entire shoulder corpus. This tests whether the dressing scene -- the most private and repeated daily pain moment -- can anchor a full native article the way the sock anchored the hip control.

**Sources:** "intense enough that I get tears in my eyes when I put on a shirt" -- Permies. "to have assistance getting dressed/undressed" -- MS Society UK. "struggling to put a jacket on" -- MS Society UK.

## ANGLE 02 -- The 2am Shoulder

**Concept:** It does not hurt during the day. It does not hurt at the gym. It waits until 2am, when she is asleep and cannot brace against it. The shoulder wakes her up every night like it has been saving the pain for the dark. *One-word: Ambush*

**Angle:** The belief shift is that nighttime shoulder pain is not random -- it is positional compression. When lying down, body weight presses the shoulder into tissue that has lost its capacity to absorb the load. The tissue is starved during the day and crushed at night. *Emotion: the betrayal of pain that hides during waking hours and attacks during rest.*

**Use Case:** The bedroom, 2:17am -- she is awake. Again. The shoulder is pulsing on the side she rolled onto. She shifts to her back. Now both shoulders ache against the mattress. She sits up and stares at the wall. She has work in five hours.

**Hypothesis:** "Can't sleep on affected side" at 14 mentions (tied for #1 in shoulder VOC). "my shoulders don't hurt in the day or at the gym but they wake me up hurting EVERY night" is the most specific and surprising verbatim. This tests whether the paradox -- pain that only comes at night -- creates a stronger curiosity hook than general pain descriptions.

**Sources:** "my shoulders don't hurt in the day or at the gym but they wake me up hurting EVERY night" -- Barbell Medicine. "pain woke me nightly" -- Reflex Health. "Sleeping was virtually impossible" -- BoneSmart.

## ANGLE 03 -- The Eight-Month Physio

**Concept:** She went three times a week for eight months. She did the exercises. She bought the bands. She used the ice. She did everything right. And on month eight, she was in the exact same place as month one. The exercises were right. The target was wrong. *One-word: Misaimed*

**Angle:** The belief shift is that rotator cuff exercises strengthen the muscle around a joint capsule that is starved and adhesed. Strength without tissue health is like tuning an engine with a blocked fuel line. The exercises are not wrong -- they are premature. *Emotion: the betrayal of compliance -- she did everything she was told and got nothing back.*

**Use Case:** The physio clinic, session thirty-two -- the band is in her hand. The same green band from month one. She pulls it to the side, feels the pinch, stops. The physio says "try to push through it." She wonders if pushing through it for eight months is enough pushing through it.

**Hypothesis:** "PT / physio didn't help" at 12 mentions (tied for 3rd highest). "I was in Physical therapy for 8 months before I got fed up" is a verbatim. This tests whether long-duration failure (8 months, not 2 weeks) creates a stronger failed-solution entry because it validates effort, not laziness.

**Sources:** "I was in Physical therapy for 8 months before I got fed up" -- Frozen Shoulder Clinic. "had a bunch of physio that said to do various rotator cuff exercises but they have done nothing" -- Barbell Medicine. "Even the physio rehab exercise cause pain" -- Barbell Medicine.

**Sources:** "unclip my bra strap" -- MS Society UK. "unable to reach behind my back" -- Reflex Health. "Simple activities like washing my hair, changing clothes, opening doors" -- Frozen Shoulder Clinic.

## ANGLE 05 -- The Frozen Diagnosis

**Concept:** The doctor said "frozen shoulder" and she heard a sentence. She should have heard a clock. Because frozen shoulder has stages, and each stage has a window, and the window she is in right now will close. What she does in this phase determines whether the next phase takes six months or three years. *One-word: Window*

**Angle:** The belief shift is that frozen shoulder is not one condition -- it is three phases (freezing, frozen, thawing). Most people are treated as if all three are the same. The tissue intervention that works in the freezing phase is different from the one in the frozen phase. Timing changes the outcome. *Emotion: urgency born from ignorance -- she did not know there was a clock, and it has been running.*

**Use Case:** The kitchen table, the evening after the diagnosis -- she is googling "how long does frozen shoulder last." The answers range from 6 months to 3 years. She does not know which phase she is in. Nobody told her there were phases.

**Hypothesis:** "Frozen shoulder / adhesive capsulitis" at 14 mentions (2nd highest condition). "suddenly suffering from frozen shoulder (adhesive capsulitis)" is a verbatim. This tests whether urgency framing (a closing window) converts better than pain framing for the newly diagnosed buyer who is still in research mode.

**Sources:** "suddenly suffering from frozen shoulder (adhesive capsulitis)" -- Frozen Shoulder Clinic. "my entire life becoming paralyzed and frozen" -- Frozen Shoulder Clinic.

## ANGLE 07 -- The Snap, Crackle, Pop

**Concept:** Every time she moves the arm, it makes a sound. Not a pain -- a sound. A click. A pop. A grinding that she can feel before she hears it. The sound is the shoulder telling her something is structurally wrong, and she has been turning up the television to avoid hearing it. *One-word: Signal*

**Angle:** The belief shift is that joint sounds are tissue signals -- the capsule is dry, the tendons are catching on surfaces they should glide over, and the tissue that lubricates and cushions the movement has deteriorated. The sound is not damage; it is friction. And friction has a cause. *Emotion: the low-grade alarm of a body that keeps warning you and you keep ignoring it.*

**Use Case:** The kitchen, morning -- she reaches for a mug from the overhead cabinet. The shoulder clicks three times on the way up. Her teenage son is eating cereal at the table and looks up. "That sounded bad, Mum." She puts the mug down with her other hand. "It's fine."

**Hypothesis:** "Snapping / cracking / popping" at 8 mentions. "As I rotate my arm it snaps, crackles, and pops" is a vivid verbatim. This tests whether the sound of the injury (audible, visceral, witnessed by others) is a stronger hook than the feeling of the injury, because sounds are shareable and relatable even to people who do not have the condition.

**Sources:** "As I rotate my arm it snaps, crackles, and pops" -- AnandTech. "painful tension builds up in the joints, then lifting my arm produces a loud popping sound" -- Patient.info.

## ANGLE 08 -- The One-Arm Life

**Concept:** She is not injured. She is not in a sling. She is not post-surgery. But she lives with one arm. The right one does what the left one used to share. Carrying, opening, reaching, cooking, driving -- all right-handed now, even though she is left-handed. *One-word: Halved*

**Angle:** The belief shift is that compensating with the other arm does not rest the shoulder -- it lets the tissue continue to deteriorate unopposed. The good arm is not a solution; it is a mask. The starved tissue gets no demand, no circulation, no signal to repair. *Emotion: the invisible disability of living at half capacity while looking completely normal to everyone around you.*

**Use Case:** The supermarket checkout -- she is loading the bags with her right arm only. The checkout person asks if she needs help. She says no. She loads the car one-handed. She drives home one-handed. Nobody at home will know.

**Hypothesis:** "Doing the simplest things without one of your shoulders... is just incredibly difficult" is a verbatim from BoneSmart. This tests whether the concept of a halved life -- not a dramatic pain moment but a constant background reduction -- converts the buyer who does not identify as "injured" but is clearly impaired.

**Sources:** "Doing the simplest things without one of your shoulders... is just incredibly difficult" -- BoneSmart. "After 3 weeks I finally learned how to shave with my left hand" -- BoneSmart.

## ANGLE 09 -- The Injection Clock

**Concept:** The cortisone bought her three months. The second one bought six weeks. The third one bought twelve days. The curve is clear. The relief is getting shorter and the appointments are getting closer together. At some point the line hits zero and the next step is not another injection. *One-word: Diminishing*

**Angle:** The belief shift is that cortisone reduces inflammation temporarily but does nothing for the tissue adhesion causing the inflammation. Each shot works for less time because the underlying tissue condition is worsening while the symptoms are being masked. *Emotion: the mathematical certainty that this will end, and what comes after injections is not more injections.*

**Use Case:** The GP office, Tuesday morning -- the doctor is writing a referral. "I can give you one more, but after that we need to talk about other options." She nods. She already knows what "other options" means. She leaves the office and sits in the car for ten minutes before starting the engine.

**Hypothesis:** "Cortisone shot wore off" at 7 mentions. "cortisone shots... nothing seemed to lessen the symptoms" is a verbatim. This tests whether the diminishing-returns angle (a curve, not a single failure) is stronger than a flat "cortisone doesn't work" message, because it introduces inevitability.

**Sources:** "cortisone shots... nothing seemed to lessen the symptoms" -- Frozen Shoulder Clinic. "ice, heat, cupping, laser, gentle stretches. This provided temporary relief at best" -- Reflex Health.

## ANGLE 10 -- The Rest That Made It Worse

**Concept:** She was told to rest it. So she rested it. Three weeks. Then six weeks. Then three months. And when she came back, the shoulder was worse than when she stopped using it. The rest did not heal it. The rest starved it. *One-word: Starved*

**Angle:** The belief shift is that rest removes demand from tissue that needs demand to heal. Without movement, circulation to the capsule drops, adhesions form, and the range shrinks. Rest is not recovery -- for shoulder tissue, it is regression. *Emotion: the anger of doing exactly what you were told and having it make you worse.*

**Use Case:** The lounge room, week seven of rest -- she has not lifted her arm above her head in forty-nine days. She reaches for the remote on the shelf above the TV. The arm stops at chest height. Seven weeks ago it reached the shelf. Rest stole what was left.

**Hypothesis:** "Rest didn't help" at 7 mentions. "3 weeks total rest also didn't help" is a verbatim. This tests whether the contrarian framing (rest is the enemy, not the cure) creates a pattern-interrupt hook that catches the buyer mid-treatment and makes her question the advice she is currently following.

**Sources:** "3 weeks total rest also didn't help" -- Barbell Medicine. "I took 5 months off at one point to see if it would heal" -- multiple forums.

**Angle:** The belief shift is that the cross-body reach for the seatbelt is one of the highest-demand movements for a compromised shoulder capsule -- it combines internal rotation, adduction, and extension simultaneously. The tissue cannot do three things at once when it can barely do one. *Emotion: the dread of a pain you know is coming, that you cannot avoid, that happens before you can even go anywhere.*

**Use Case:** The car, driveway, 8:15am -- she is in the driver's seat. The seatbelt is behind her right shoulder. She takes a breath. She reaches. The shoulder lights up from joint to elbow. She clicks the belt in and exhales. The drive has not started yet.

**Hypothesis:** "Driving / fastening seatbelt" at 7 mentions. "fasten a seatbelt, drive a car" is in a list of disrupted activities. This tests whether a micro-moment (3 seconds of pain) can anchor an entire ad, because the seatbelt reach is universal, daily, and unavoidable.

**Sources:** "vacuum, reach to clean, fasten a seatbelt, drive a car" -- Reflex Health. "Driving / fastening seatbelt" at 7 mentions across sources.
